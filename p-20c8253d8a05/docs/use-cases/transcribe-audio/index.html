Transcribe speech to text

What you get

Section titled “What you get”

The words spoken in a recording, written out on your own machine: inga transcribe turns a WAV, MP3 or FLAC file into text, SubRip or WebVTT subtitles, or JSON with each segment’s start and end, with OpenAI’s Whisper models in whisper.cpp’s files, NVIDIA’s Parakeet models (see Parakeet) or Cohere Transcribe (see Cohere Transcribe). Whisper detects the language (100 of them for the multilingual models) or takes the one you give, and can translate the speech to English. inga serve offers the same model to OpenAI clients: POST /v1/audio/transcriptions and /v1/audio/translations. The text can also come while the audio does, from a file or a microphone (streamed), and with any of these models Inga can say who speaks when (speakers).

This guide is about turning speech into text. To ask a chat model questions about a recording (summaries, answers), see ask-about-audio.md.

Requirements

Section titled “Requirements”
  • Windows, Linux or macOS; any x86-64 or ARM64 CPU (AVX2 or AVX-512 make it much faster).
  • A Whisper model in whisper.cpp’s format (ggml-*.bin) or as a GGUF file converted by transcribe.cpp (handy-computer/whisper-*-gguf). Whisper large-v3-turbo, the one this guide uses, is 1.6 GB in F16 (ggml-large-v3-turbo.bin), 874 MB in Q8_0 and 547 MB in Q5_0; it needs about its size plus 1 GB of free RAM. The small models (ggml-base.bin, 148 MB; ggml-small.bin, 488 MB) are faster and less accurate; the .en ones know English only. Or a Parakeet model as a GGUF file converted by transcribe.cpp (handy-computer/parakeet-*-gguf; the 0.6B models are about 2.4 GB in F32, half that in F16), see Parakeet. Or Cohere Transcribe (handy-computer/cohere-transcribe-03-2026-gguf, 2.4 GB in Q8_0, 1.6 GB in Q4_K_M), see Cohere Transcribe.
  • Network access for the download (see run-a-model-from-hugging-face.md).

1. Get Inga

Section titled “1. Get Inga”

Download the archive for your system from the Releases page and put inga on your PATH (details); inga --version checks it.

3. Transcribe

Section titled “3. Transcribe”

The text goes to the standard output, a summary to the standard error:

Recordings of any length (up to 30 minutes) are read 30 seconds at a time, each window starting where the previous one’s last sentence ended, as whisper.cpp does. The language is detected from the first 30 seconds; give it with --language it when you know it (faster, and right from the first word).

4. Subtitles, timestamps and JSON

Section titled “4. Subtitles, timestamps and JSON”

--output vtt writes WebVTT subtitles, --output json OpenAI’s verbose_json: the task, the language, the duration, the text and every segment with its times, tokens, average log-probability and no-speech probability.

When each word is said

Section titled “When each word is said”

--word-timestamps times every word as whisper.cpp’s --dtw does: once a window is decoded, its text is read again by the decoder and the cross-attention of the model’s alignment heads (whisper.cpp’s presets: tiny … large-v3-turbo) is aligned to the audio by dynamic time warping. A word starts when its first token is said and ends when the next word starts (the last one at the end of its segment). With --output json the words are in words (word, start, end, probability); with --timestamps the text has one line per word. It costs one more decoder pass per window.

5. Translate to English

Section titled “5. Translate to English”

Whisper’s translate task writes English text whatever the language spoken. Note that large-v3-turbo was trained with little translation data: its translations are weaker than large-v3’s or medium’s.

7. Parakeet

Section titled “7. Parakeet”

NVIDIA’s Parakeet models are smaller and faster than Whisper large, with punctuation and capitals (TDT v2 / v3), and time every word by itself. transcribe.cpp converts them to GGUF files (general.architecture = parakeet); Inga picks the model by that architecture, so the commands above do not change:

What differs from Whisper:

  • The whole recording goes through the encoder at once (no 30-second windows), as NeMo and transcribe.cpp do: memory and time grow with its length (the attention is quadratic), so a long recording is slower per minute than a short one.
  • The decoding is greedy: TDT (v2, v3, 1.1B: each token says how many 80 ms frames to move on), RNN-T or CTC. --beam-size, --best-of, --temp and --prompt are Whisper’s and are not used.
  • Words are timed by the encoder frames their tokens were read at (--word-timestamps adds them to the JSON at no extra cost); segments end after ., ? or !, at a pause of a second, or before 30 seconds.
  • Parakeet does not detect or report the language (language auto for the multilingual v3); --language is checked against the file’s list. Nemotron 3.5 ASR Streaming reads it as its prompt (--language en-US, --language de, …; automatic by default) and writes a language tag Inga reports.
  • No translation: --translate and /v1/audio/translations are refused.
  • The streaming models (Nemotron Speech Streaming, Nemotron 3.5) transcribe whole files with their chunked attention, or stream (below).

Serving works the same: inga serve parakeet-tdt-0.6b-v3-Q8_0.gguf, or --whisper FILE with a Parakeet file next to a chat model.

Streamed: the text while the audio comes

Section titled “Streamed: the text while the audio comes”

A cache-aware streaming model (chunked attention, causal convolutions: Nemotron Speech Streaming, Nemotron 3.5 ASR Streaming) transcribes chunk by chunk: each chunk of encoder frames goes through the encoder with the rows it needs from the chunks before (cached), and the decoder goes on from where it stopped. The text grows while the audio comes, and at the end it is the same as the whole recording transcribed at once.

- reads raw PCM from the standard input (without --stream, all of it, then the transcript). Other models stream too, by transcribing the audio received again every second (the summary line says so): fine for a sentence, slow for a lecture.

Over HTTP:

  • stream=true in /v1/audio/transcriptions answers OpenAI’s events, transcript.text.delta while the text grows and transcript.text.done at the end (with response_format json or text).
  • GET /api/inga/transcriptions/live is a WebSocket for live audio: send binary messages of 16 kHz mono 16-bit little-endian PCM and {"type": "end"} at the end of each utterance; the server answers {"type": "partial", "text"} while it hears and {"type": "done", "text", "language", "duration"}. The language is the ?language= of the URL. A browser sends the API key as the subprotocol inga-key. + the key’s bytes in hexadecimal, beside inga. The web UI’s voice mode uses it (see Talk to a model by voice).

8. Only the speech: voice activity detection

Section titled “8. Only the speech: voice activity detection”

A recording with long silences (a meeting, a lecture, a voicemail) transcribes faster and with fewer invented sentences when only its speech is decoded. Inga does it as whisper.cpp’s --vad does, with the same Silero model file:

The Silero model gives a speech probability for every 32 ms of audio; the windows above --vad-threshold (0.5) make the speech segments, which end after --vad-min-silence-duration-ms (100) of silence, are dropped when shorter than --vad-min-speech-duration-ms (250), are split when longer than --vad-max-speech-duration-s (unlimited), merged when less than 200 ms apart and padded by --vad-speech-pad-ms (30). The segments are joined with 0.1 s of silence between them (each keeps --vad-samples-overlap, 0.1 s, of what follows), the model transcribes that, and every time in the result (segments, words, subtitles) is mapped back to the original recording. A recording with no speech gives no segments. The option names and defaults are whisper-cli’s (-vm, -vt, -vspd, -vsd, -vmsd, -vp, -vo too); --vad-model turns --vad on. It works with Parakeet as well.

Over HTTP the server loads the model once (inga serve ggml-large-v3-turbo.bin --vad-model ggml-silero-v6.2.0.bin) and a request asks for it with whisper-server’s fields: vad=true, vad_threshold, vad_min_speech_duration_ms, vad_min_silence_duration_ms, vad_max_speech_duration_s, vad_speech_pad_ms, vad_samples_overlap. --vad on the server turns it on for every request that does not say vad=false; the other --vad-* options set the defaults.

9. Whisper from a Hugging Face repository

Section titled “9. Whisper from a Hugging Face repository”

The openai/whisper-* repositories, distil-whisper and the fine-tunes published as WhisperForConditionalGeneration load as they are, without a conversion:

Inga reads config.json, model.safetensors (F32, F16 or BF16), the vocabulary of tokenizer.json and the mel filters of preprocessor_config.json, and presents them as the ggml-*.bin file whisper.cpp’s convert-h5-to-ggml.py would write: the transcript is the same as with that file.

10. Cohere Transcribe

Section titled “10. Cohere Transcribe”

Cohere Transcribe 03-2026 (about 2B parameters, Apache-2.0) is among the most accurate open speech-to-text models: English, French, German, Spanish, Italian, Portuguese, Dutch, Polish, Greek, Arabic, Japanese, Chinese, Vietnamese and Korean, with punctuation and capitals. transcribe.cpp converts it to GGUF files (general.architecture = cohere_asr); Inga also reads Cohere’s own Hugging Face directory (CohereLabs/cohere-transcribe-03-2026, gated: accept its form, then inga pull it with a token). The Arabic variant (cohere-transcribe-arabic-07-2026, Arabic and English) runs the same way.

What differs from Whisper and Parakeet:

  • The language is not detected: give it with --language (it, it-IT or italian); without it the model transcribes as English. Over HTTP a request without language uses the first language of its Accept-Language header the model knows: the web UI’s voice mode sends the browser’s language this way.
  • No timestamps: the model writes text only. Inga cuts a recording longer than 35 seconds (Cohere’s advice) at its quietest moment near the end of each 35 seconds; each piece is one segment, so subtitles show a piece at a time. --word-timestamps is refused, and so is --translate.
  • The encoder is Parakeet’s (48 blocks of 1280: the slowest model of this guide on a CPU); the decoder is greedy, with transcribe.cpp’s guard against a loop.
  • --stream works by transcribing the audio received again (it is not a streaming model).

In the web UI, the Hearing: transcription model panel of Models → Voice suggests the Q8_0 file; any Cohere file in your models folder is listed there too, and in the library’s Voice: transcription box with Use for voice.

11. Who speaks when: Sortformer

Section titled “11. Who speaks when: Sortformer”

A meeting or an interview reads better with its speakers. NVIDIA’s Sortformer finds who speaks in every 80 ms of the recording (up to four speakers, numbered by when they first talk), and Inga gives each segment of the transcript its speaker, with any model of this guide:

  • The file: NVIDIA publishes Sortformer as a .nemo checkpoint (nvidia/diar_streaming_sortformer_4spk-v2, NVIDIA Open Model License; v1, diar_sortformer_4spk-v1, is CC-BY-NC-4.0). tools/convert-sortformer.py (NumPy and PyYAML, no PyTorch; the checkpoint’s pickle is read without running any code from it) writes the GGUF file Inga reads; --f16 halves it, --license records the licence, which Inga prints with the model. The streaming v2 model runs offline here, on the whole recording.
  • The output: [S1] before each line of the text, the subtitles (--output srt, vtt) and the timestamped lines (--timestamps); "speaker": "S1" on each segment of --output json. Whisper’s and Parakeet’s segments are cut where the speaker of their words changes (the word times place the change; Inga computes them even without --word-timestamps, and shows them only when asked); Cohere Transcribe has no word times, so each of its pieces takes the speaker who talks most in it.
  • The options: --speakers N keeps at most the N who talk most (default 4); --diarizer-threshold X (0.5) is the probability above which a speaker talks in a frame. Turns shorter than 0.2 s are dropped and gaps shorter than 0.25 s between two turns of one speaker are filled, as NeMo’s post-processing does.
  • Over HTTP: inga serve ggml-large-v3-turbo.bin --diarizer sortformer-4spk-v2.gguf (or --whisper M --diarizer ... next to a chat model): every transcription gets its speakers (speaker in verbose_json, [S1] in srt, vtt and text) unless the request says diarize=false (whisper-server’s field).
  • --stream does not take --diarizer: transcribe the whole file to know who speaks.

--language L the spoken language (en, it, de, …; default auto: detected by Whisper; English for Cohere Transcribe)

--best-of N candidates sampled at each higher temperature (default 5)
--word-timestamps when each word is said: words in JSON, one line per word with --timestamps
--stream the text written as it is transcribed (- as the file: PCM from the standard input, read as it comes)
--vad-model F, --vad transcribe only the speech a Silero model finds (--vad-threshold, --vad-min-speech-duration-ms, --vad-min-silence-duration-ms, --vad-max-speech-duration-s, --vad-speech-pad-ms, --vad-samples-overlap)
--diarizer M who speaks when: a Sortformer model gives each segment its speaker (--speakers N, default 4; --diarizer-threshold X, default 0.5)
-t, --threads N CPU threads
--whisper M (serve) a speech-to-text model (Whisper, Parakeet or Cohere Transcribe) next to the language models

How it works

Section titled “How it works”

Inga runs Whisper as whisper.cpp’s whisper-cli does by default (--beam-size 5 --best-of 5): the audio is resampled to 16 kHz mono and turned into a log-mel spectrogram with the file’s mel filters; the encoder reads 30 seconds; the decoder starts from <|startoftranscript|>, the language and the task, and writes text and timestamp tokens with OpenAI’s rules (timestamps in pairs and increasing, blank and special tokens suppressed). At temperature 0 five beams run side by side: each step draws five candidate tokens from each beam’s probabilities (whisper.cpp’s way, with the same random numbers) and keeps the five most probable sequences; the beam with the best average log-probability wins. A window whose text is unlikely or repetitive is decoded again at a higher temperature, by five sampled candidates; one whose <|nospeech|> probability is high is skipped as silence. The previous window’s text guides the next one. On synthetic models Inga gives the tokens, segments and word times whisper.cpp gives (crates/inga-vision/tests/whisper.rs).

Parakeet is computed as NeMo computes it (transcribe.cpp’s port is the reference): NeMo’s log-mel features (pre-emphasis, a 512-point FFT, Slaney filters, each bin normalized), three stride-2 convolutions down to one row per 80 ms, the FastConformer blocks (half feed-forward, self-attention with relative positions, a convolution module, half feed-forward), then the greedy decoder. On synthetic files Inga gives the words and frames transcribe.cpp gives (crates/inga-vision/src/parakeet.rs, tools/parakeet-oracle.md).

Cohere Transcribe is computed as transcribe.cpp computes it (itself checked against Hugging Face Transformers): Parakeet’s features and encoder over every frame, a projection to the decoder, then a Transformer decoder (a stored position table, self-attention with a cache, cross-attention to the encoder, ReLU) from the ten-token prompt ▁ <|startofcontext|> <|startoftranscript|> <|emo:undefined|> <|it|> <|it|> <|pnc|> <|noitn|> <|notimestamp|> <|nodiarize|>, greedy to the end token. On synthetic files its encoder gives Parakeet’s rows and its decoder a plain reference’s logits (crates/inga-vision/src/cohere.rs).

Silero VAD is computed as whisper.cpp computes it: each window of 512 samples padded by reflection, its short-time Fourier transform as a convolution, four convolutions, an LSTM whose state runs across the windows, a sigmoid; then whisper.cpp’s segment rules and time mapping. On a synthetic Silero model Inga gives whisper.cpp’s probabilities (within 1e-4), segments and transcripts (crates/inga-vision/tests/vad.rs).

Sortformer is computed as NeMo’s SortformerEncLabelModel computes it offline: Parakeet’s features and FastConformer encoder, a projection, a post-norm Transformer encoder (18 layers in NVIDIA’s model), then ReLU, a hidden layer, ReLU and a sigmoid per speaker for every 80 ms frame. On synthetic files it gives a plain f64 reference’s probabilities, and its turns are those of tools/sortformer_oracle.py (NeMo’s binarization and filtering written with NumPy; crates/inga-vision/src/sortformer.rs).

Troubleshooting

Section titled “Troubleshooting”
  • “is not a speech-to-text model”: --model must be a whisper.cpp ggml-*.bin file, a Whisper, Parakeet or Cohere Transcribe GGUF file or a Hugging Face Whisper or Cohere Transcribe directory, not a chat model or a projector (mmproj-*.gguf).
  • “is a voice activity detection model”: a ggml-silero-*.bin file goes to --vad-model, next to a speech-to-text --model.
  • “voice activity detection needs a Silero model”: --vad (or vad=true in a request) needs --vad-model.
  • “a Parakeet model transcribes, it does not translate” (or “a Cohere Transcribe model”): translate with a Whisper model.
  • “Cohere Transcribe gives no timestamps”: drop --word-timestamps (or timestamp_granularities=word), or use Whisper or Parakeet for word times.
  • “language fr: Cohere Transcribe … transcribes en, ar”: the Arabic variant knows two languages; take cohere-transcribe-03-2026 for the others.
  • “audio: not a known audio format”: the file is not WAV, MP3 or FLAC; convert it, for example ffmpeg -i video.mp4 -ar 16000 -ac 1 audio.wav.
  • “this Whisper model knows English only”: a .en model neither detects languages nor translates; take a multilingual one.
  • Repeated or invented sentences on silence or music: give --language, keep the default temperature, or cut the silent parts; Whisper tends to write something for every 30 seconds.
  • Slow: a Q5_0 or Q8_0 file and -t with your physical core count help; the smaller models (ggml-small.bin, ggml-base.bin) are several times faster.
