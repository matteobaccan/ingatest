Listen to a radio of music made on your computer

What you get

Section titled “What you get”

A radio that plays music without end, made on your computer by the Music page’s model (ACE-Step 1.5 turbo, MIT; make-music.md), in stations of your choice: instrumental or sung, in the language you choose, with the news of a site if you want them. Every track is new when the computer keeps up; when it is slower than the music plays, the radio mixes in the tracks it made before, and says how much of each hour is new. Tracks follow each other with a crossfade, all at the same loudness. Everyone who listens hears the same programme: the web UI’s Radio page, VLC, foobar2000, a phone’s player.

Requirements

Section titled “Requirements”
  • inga serve (the web UI), and the music model’s files: 6.3 GB, downloaded once on the Music page (Prepare the music) or with inga pull kit://ace-step. Without them the Radio page says what to download and leads to the Music page.
  • A graphics card the model fits on makes the music faster than it plays; on a CPU the radio works too, and says its speed (below).
  • For songs with singing and for summarised news: a chat model loaded (the Models page). Without one, a sung station plays instrumental tracks and the news read their titles; the page says so.
  • For the news read aloud: a voice (Models, then Voice: a Piper voice or OuteTTS).

Start it

Section titled “Start it”
  1. In the web UI, Music, then Radio under it in the side bar (beside Songs, the Music page, and Player for the phone).
  2. Listen. The radio turns on; the first track of a station is 30 seconds long, so the first sound comes soon; the next ones are made while it plays.

Or start it with the server: inga serve --radio (or enabled = true under [radio] in inga.toml).

The front panel

Section titled “The front panel”

The top of the page is a radio: the station’s name, its place in the list (“Station 1 of 4”) and what it plays; the track playing (“Rock · 96 BPM · sung in Italian”, new or from your library and how many times it played) with where it is; level bars that follow the sound while it plays (still when the computer asks for reduced motion). Below them:

  • Back plays the station’s track before again; Next fades into the next one.
  • The arrows on both sides go to the previous and the next station; Station lists them all.
  • Listen plays the radio on this page; Mute silences it here.
  • Like keeps the track playing (the library never removes it, and plays it twice as often).

On, here, other devices

Section titled “On, here, other devices”

Three switches, each said plainly on the page:

  • The radio: Turn on and Turn off. Off, the computer makes no music and nobody hears it.
  • Here: listening or muted on this page. Muted, the radio keeps making music (and other devices keep hearing it).
  • Other devices: Stop other devices ends the stream of every player on another device and refuses new ones; this computer keeps listening. A listener counts as this computer when it connects from a loopback address (127.0.0.1, ::1): the page opened on the computer running Inga. Allow other devices lets them in again.

Stations

Section titled “Stations”

Stations lists them, the one you listen to first marked. Each has a name, its styles (played in turn: Lofi, Ambient, Jazz piano, Chill electronic, Acoustic, Classical strings, Rock, Indie rock, Pop, Blues, Funk, Synthwave, or your own words such as “dark techno”), whether its songs are sung and in which language, and its news. The radio starts with four: Chill (instrumental), Rock and Pop (sung in English) and Classical. Add a station, Edit and Delete change the list; a station’s tracks stay in the library when it is deleted. Only the station you listen to gets new tracks: the others are not fed, so the computer works for what you hear.

A sung station’s lyrics are written, track by track, by the chat model loaded, in the station’s language; the page says which model writes them. With no chat model loaded the station’s tracks are instrumental until one is, and the page says so.

The news

Section titled “The news”

In a station’s Edit: News at the end of a track, the address of an RSS or Atom feed (most news sites have one: look for “RSS” on the site), every how many tracks, and the voice that reads them (auto: the voice model’s own). The radio reads the feed at most every 10 minutes (the site’s robots.txt first: a site that does not allow it is not read, and the page says so), takes its three latest items not read yet, and, with a chat model loaded, summarises each in one sentence in the station’s language (without one: their titles). The voice reads them between two chimes at the end of a track, always saying the source, and ends by saying the music is made by AI.

The library

Section titled “The library”

Library lists every track made: its name, its station, its length, when it was made and how many times it played, with the totals (the tracks, the minutes of music, the disk they take). Play plays one alone in a small player (the radio is muted here meanwhile, and keeps making music); Like and Delete are beside it.

What the radio did

Section titled “What the radio did”

A scrollable list of every decision: the station tuned, each track made (how long it took and on which device), each one played, the silence cut at a track’s end, the news read, why the generator waits. The same lines are kept in profiles/default/radio/log.txt of Inga’s home, each with its date and time (past 1 MiB the file starts again, the old one kept as log.1.txt).

A track sometimes ends with silence: the radio cuts the silence at its end (under −50 dBFS over 100 ms), and a silent hole of 1.5 s or more in its last third ends the track there, with a short fade. The list says what was cut.

How fast it makes music

Section titled “How fast it makes music”

How fast it makes music, measured on the last ten tracks: how many times faster than real time it makes music and on which device when it keeps up; when it does not, how many of every 60 minutes are new (“the rest comes from your library”), and the reason (on the CPU: a graphics card the model fits on would make it faster). The track being made and its step are said beside it.

Listen in another player

Section titled “Listen in another player”

Playlist (.m3u) downloads a playlist that VLC, foobar2000 or a phone’s player opens; Copy the link copies the stream’s address. Both carry a listening token of their own, which opens only the stream, the playlist and the library’s tracks: players send no API key, and a server with one stays closed to the rest. POST /api/inga/radio {"action": "new_link"} makes a new token, and the old link stops working.

The stream is /radio/stream: FLAC by default, lossless, 48 kHz stereo; ?format=wav gives WAV (about 1.5 Mbit/s) for a player that does not read an endless FLAC. The Radio page switches to WAV by itself when the browser does not play the FLAC stream, and says so. Every track is labelled as made by AI: its file’s tags (AI_GENERATED=true, the model and its licence), a watermark in its sound (inga-audio-mark-1), and the stream’s own tags; the radio is for personal use.

Sharing the computer

Section titled “Sharing the computer”

The radio’s tracks are the last job of the machine: a song of the Music page, an image or a model load go first (the radio’s jobs queue behind every other kind of the machine’s heavy jobs), and a track being made gives way to them and to a chat being answered, stopping before its next step, once three minutes of music are ready ahead; it is made again later, and the page says why the generator waits. Its thread runs at the operating system’s low priority. The radio stops by itself after 30 minutes with no listener (idle_stop), so the computer does not make music for nobody.

Settings

Section titled “Settings”

The keys of [radio] in inga.toml (docs/config.md): styles (the first station’s, at the first start), name, track_seconds ("90-180"), ahead_minutes (20), crossfade_seconds (4), loudness (−16 LUFS), no_repeat_hours (6), library_max ("2G"), idle_stop (30), format (flac), and news.voice (auto: the voice of a new station’s news). The stations are kept in profiles/default/radio/stations.json of Inga’s home, the tracks in profiles/default/radio/tracks/, each a FLAC with its record (<id>.json: the station, the style, the prompt, the lyrics, the seed, the model, how fast and where it was made, its loudness, the silence cut), apart from the Music page’s songs; past library_max the most played go first, never a liked one.

From the API

Section titled “From the API”

GET /api/inga/radio (on or off, the stations and the one listened to, what plays, what is next, the track being made, the speed, the news, the listeners, the stream’s links), POST /api/inga/radio ({"action": "start"}, stop, skip, back, new_link, {"station": "rock"}, {"stations": [...]}, {"remote": false}, {"styles": [...]}), GET /api/inga/radio/library, POST and DELETE /api/inga/radio/library/{id} ({"liked": true}), GET /api/inga/radio/library/{id}/audio, GET /api/inga/radio/log, GET /radio/stream and GET /radio.m3u (docs/api/inga.yaml, tag radio).
