Generate images from text

What you get

Section titled “What you get”

A picture from a sentence, on the CPU: inga image writes a PNG file with Stable Diffusion 1.x, 2.x, SDXL, SD-Turbo, SDXL-Turbo, SD 3.x, Flux.1 (dev, schnell) or Qwen-Image, and inga serve offers the same model to the browser (an image mode in the chat page) and to OpenAI clients (/v1/images/generations). For the same checkpoint, prompt, size, seed and settings you get the image stable-diffusion.cpp and the AUTOMATIC1111 web UI give. To redraw a picture of yours, or only a part of it, see edit-images.md.

Requirements

Section titled “Requirements”
  • Windows, Linux or macOS; any x86-64 or ARM64 CPU (AVX2 or AVX-512 make it much faster).
  • A checkpoint in one file: a .safetensors file in the original layout (what the AUTOMATIC1111 web UI and ComfyUI load) or a GGUF file converted by stable-diffusion.cpp; or a model in parts (a ComfyUI-GGUF UNet with its VAE and CLIP files, a diffusers folder; see “Models in parts” below). Stable Diffusion 2.x and SD-Turbo have 1.3 billion weights (2.6 GB in F16, twice that in F32), Stable Diffusion 1.x 1.1 billion; drawing 512 x 512 pictures takes about the file’s size plus 2 to 3 GB of free RAM. SDXL and SDXL-Turbo have 3.5 billion weights (6.9 GB in F16); count the file’s size plus 4 to 8 GB of free RAM for 1024 x 1024 pictures.
  • Network access for the download (see run-a-model-from-hugging-face.md); some repositories ask you to accept their licence on huggingface.co first, then to set HF_TOKEN.

1. Get Inga

Section titled “1. Get Inga”

Download the archive for your system from the Releases page and put inga on your PATH (details); inga --version checks it.

2. Get a model

Section titled “2. Get a model”

SD-Turbo draws a usable picture in one step, the fastest choice on a CPU. Name the checkpoint file of its repository:

Other checkpoints work the same way, for example a Stable Diffusion 1.5 file (hf://<org>/<repo>/<file>.safetensors), SDXL (hf://stabilityai/stable-diffusion-xl-base-1.0/sd_xl_base_1.0.safetensors) and SDXL-Turbo (hf://stabilityai/sdxl-turbo/sd_xl_turbo_1.0_fp16.safetensors), or a GGUF conversion (hf://<org>/<repo>:Q8_0 or /<file>.gguf): quantized GGUF files are smaller and their matrices run on the quantized kernels of the language models. A checkpoint already on disk is used by its path. Models in parts are read too: see “Models in parts” below.

3. Draw

Section titled “3. Draw”

A file whose name contains turbo draws in 1 step without guidance; other checkpoints take 20 steps with a guidance scale of 7, as stable-diffusion.cpp does. Pictures are 512 x 512 by default, 1024 x 1024 for SDXL (its training size; SDXL-Turbo’s is 512 x 512). To repeat an image, give its seed with --seed; to change it, change the seed or the prompt:

On Windows (PowerShell) put the command on one line, or end the lines with a backtick instead of \.

The prompt takes the AUTOMATIC1111 syntax: (words) weighs them up by 1.1, [words] down, (words:1.5) by 1.5, and BREAK starts a new chunk of 75 tokens (longer prompts are cut into chunks by themselves).

Models in parts

Section titled “Models in parts”

Some repositories ship the UNet alone (ComfyUI-GGUF’s quantized UNets) and leave the VAE and the text encoders to files of their own, as stable-diffusion.cpp’s --diffusion-model, --vae, --clip_l and --clip_g take them. Name each part:

Inga first says which file gives which part (--vae: sdxl_vae.safetensors, …).

  • The UNet may also be the model itself (inga image unet.gguf --vae ... --clip-l ...). Stable Diffusion 1.x / 2.x take --clip-l only (their one text encoder); SDXL takes both.
  • The VAE given with --vae replaces the one of a whole checkpoint, for instance SDXL’s fp16-fix VAE: inga image sd_xl_base_1.0.safetensors --vae sdxl_vae.safetensors -p ....
  • Each part may be in the original names, in diffusers’ (down_blocks.*, decoder.up_blocks.*) or, for the text encoders, in Hugging Face’s (text_model.*, ComfyUI’s clip_l.safetensors / clip_g.safetensors).
  • A diffusers folder (model_index.json with unet/, vae/, text_encoder/ and, for SDXL, text_encoder_2/) is one model: inga image ./stable-diffusion-xl-base-1.0 -p .... The *.fp16.safetensors files are taken when the folder has them.
  • A part that is missing is named in the error, with the option that gives it.
  • inga serve takes the same options (inga serve --diffusion-model unet.gguf --vae ... --clip-l ... --clip-g ...).

Get a model’s files together

Section titled “Get a model’s files together”

Flux, SD 3.x, Qwen-Image and Wan take their transformer, VAE and text encoders as files of their own, kept in several repositories. A kit downloads them together:

The kits (inga search WORDS lists their files with their references):

  • qwen-image (~21.5 GB): city96’s Qwen-Image-gguf (:Q4_K_M), Comfy-Org’s qwen_image_vae.safetensors, and Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct :Q8_0 from unsloth as --llm.
  • flux-schnell (~12.5 GB): city96’s FLUX.1-schnell-gguf (:Q4_K_S), FLUX.1’s ae.safetensors (Comfy-Org’s copy beside Z-Image Turbo: the same bytes as black-forest-labs’ gated one, no licence to accept), clip_l.safetensors (comfyanonymous’ flux_text_encoders) as --clip-l, city96’s t5-v1_1-xxl-encoder-gguf :Q8_0 as --t5xxl.
  • flux-dev (~12.5 GB): city96’s FLUX.1-dev-gguf (:Q4_K_S), the rest as flux-schnell (the VAE is the same file).
  • sd3.5-medium (~16.5 GB): Stability AI’s sd3.5_medium.safetensors (its VAE inside) and the clip_l, clip_g and t5xxl_fp16 files of the same repository’s text_encoders/.
  • wan2.1-t2v-1.3b (~9.1 GB, inga video): Comfy-Org’s wan2.1_t2v_1.3B_fp16.safetensors and wan_2.1_vae.safetensors, city96’s umt5-xxl-encoder-gguf :Q8_0 as --t5xxl.
  • sd3.5-large and sd3.5-large-turbo (~27.9 GB each): Stability AI’s sd3.5_large.safetensors / sd3.5_large_turbo.safetensors (16.5 GB of 16-bit weights, the VAE inside) with SD 3.5 Medium’s three text encoders, the same files (their SHA-256 compared on 2026-11-02), so one download serves the three kits. Large Turbo draws in 4 steps without guidance. Gated (auto): accept the licence of the model and of SD 3.5 Medium.

Models in one file (a checkpoint with its VAE and text encoders inside) are kits of one file too, each pinned to the commit its facts were read at:

  • sd-turbo (5.2 GB) and sdxl-turbo (6.9 GB): Stability AI’s, one step, 512 × 512. Neither repository is gated any more (Hugging Face’s gated field, 2026-11-02).
  • sdxl-lightning-4step and sdxl-lightning-8step (6.9 GB each): ByteDance’s all-in-one sdxl_lightning_4step.safetensors / _8step, 1024 × 1024, 4 or 8 steps without guidance (Inga reads the steps from the file’s name). Its card samples with “trailing” timesteps; Inga’s schedule runs from 999 to 0: unverified on the real file.
  • juggernaut-xl (7.1 GB, RunDiffusion’s Juggernaut XL v9) and realvisxl (6.9 GB, SG161222’s RealVisXL V5.0, fp16): SDXL finetunes for photos according to their makers. Their cards suggest 30 or more steps of a DPM++ sampler; Inga draws with 20 steps of Euler ancestral at a CFG of 7 unless asked (--steps, --cfg).
  • dreamshaper-8 (2.1 GB, Lykon’s DreamShaper 8): a Stable Diffusion 1.5 finetune, 512 × 512, the smallest download.

Not a kit: Qwen-Image Lightning. lightx2v publishes it as LoRA files (Qwen-Image-Lightning-8steps-V2.0.safetensors), and Inga applies no LoRA to an image model; the merged checkpoints on the Hub are a 41 GB bf16 diffusers transformer in five parts (linoyts/Qwen-Image-Lightning-8steps-fused, no licence stated) or files of Qwen-Image 2.1, another model, so none fits a kit today.

Choosing one

Section titled “Choosing one”

The Models page’s Images section lists every image kit with what helps to choose it, all from GET /api/inga/kits (the catalogue is crates/inga-hub/src/kits.rs):

  • What it does: Create, Transform, Touch up (the Images page’s Inpaint) and Extend for every model whose VAE has an encoder in Inga (Extend is an inpainting of the grown canvas); Qwen-Image creates only. Upscale: the Real-ESRGAN x4plus kit (kit://realesrgan-x4plus, 67 MB, BSD-3-Clause), shown under the Upscale filter. Edit by instruction and Reference need a model the catalogue does not have (the quick choice Edit a photo with words says so).
  • Speed: its default steps, lightning (1 to 4), fast (8 to 12) or careful (20 to 50); the time of its last picture on this machine once it drew one (kept in ~/.cache/inga/measured.json beside the chat models’ speeds). The time of a 1024 × 1024 picture on the CI’s CPU is unmeasured.
  • Fits this machine: the download, the memory it needs (its files and what the server counts for a picture of its side) against the memory free, and no GPU needed (Inga draws on the CPU).
  • Strengths, each with its source: what a model’s card says is shown as “according to its makers”, with the card’s words on hover; nothing is said measured.
  • Native side, the prompt’s languages (its card’s, or its text encoders’) and the licence: free (Apache 2.0, OpenRAIL), free under a revenue limit (Stability AI’s Community License: under US $1,000,000 a year) or non-commercial (FLUX.1-dev).
  • Access: a gated file says so on the card; Download first shows what will be downloaded and its licence, and for a gated kit asks the Hub whether this machine may download it (GET /api/inga/access: without a token, or with one whose account has not accepted the licence, Download stays off and says what to do), so the download never stops midway at a gated file.

Four quick choices sit on top: the fastest on this machine, realistic photos, text in the picture, edit a photo with words; then filters by what it does, speed, strength, licence, no account needed and fits this machine.

  • kit://NAME:QUANT takes another quantization of a transformer given by quantization (inga pull kit://qwen-image:Q8_0); the text encoders keep theirs.
  • Every file is listed on the Hub first: one that is not there (a quantization the repository lacks, a gated repository without HF_TOKEN) stops the kit before any download, naming its entry. The disk is checked for all of them together; files already in the cache are not downloaded again.
  • Stability AI’s SD 3.5 repositories are gated (the Flux kits’ VAE is not: they read Comfy-Org’s copy, the same bytes as black-forest-labs’ gated one): accept the licence on huggingface.co, then set HF_TOKEN (or run huggingface-cli login).
  • Pulling one file of a kit (inga pull hf://city96/Qwen-Image-gguf:Q4_K_M) says which of the kit’s other files are missing, and inga info FILE on a kit’s file (the transformer, the VAE, a text encoder) says what it is, where its other files are on this machine (or their references) and the command that runs them. The web UI’s model window (Info) shows the same, with Download the missing files.
  • A language model named like an image model is not one: a qwen3vl file called Qwen-Image-2.1-Turbo-Q8_0.gguf chats and does not draw. inga run, inga serve, inga info, inga image and the model window say so, with the kit to get instead.
  • inga search WORDS lists the kits’ files its words name (inga search qwen image); see find-models-and-check-support.md.

Stable Diffusion 3.x

Section titled “Stable Diffusion 3.x”

SD 3 Medium and SD 3.5 (Large, Large Turbo, Medium) come as one file with the MMDiT and the VAE; their three text encoders are inside it (sd3_medium_incl_clips_t5xxlfp16. safetensors) or in files of their own (clip_l.safetensors, clip_g.safetensors, t5xxl_fp16.safetensors):

  • SD 3.x samples with Euler along its flow schedule (shift 3), 28 steps at guidance 4.5 by default (a file named *turbo*, such as SD 3.5 Large Turbo: 4 steps without guidance), 1024 x 1024, multiples of 16 up to the model’s position table (192 patches a side: 3072 pixels for SD 3 Medium).
  • The prompt goes to the three encoders, 77 tokens per chunk; the weighted syntax works for each of them.
  • Diffusers folders of SD 3 and SD 3.5’s skip-layer guidance are not read.

Flux

Section titled “Flux”

Flux.1 ships in four files: the transformer (flux1-dev.safetensors, flux1-schnell.safetensors, or a GGUF of it such as city96’s flux1-dev-Q4_K_S.gguf), the VAE (ae.safetensors), CLIP-L (clip_l.safetensors) and T5 v1.1 XXL (t5xxl_fp16.safetensors, or a GGUF of the encoder):

  • Flux samples with Euler along its shifted schedule, without classifier-free guidance (--cfg 1): FLUX.1-dev takes its guidance as an input instead (--guidance, 3.5 by default) and 20 steps; FLUX.1-schnell 4 steps. Pictures are 1024 x 1024 by default, multiples of 16.
  • --cfg above 1 also runs the negative prompt (twice the work per step).
  • The T5 tokenizer’s vocabulary ships inside inga; T5 reads the prompt in chunks of 256 tokens, and the weighted syntax (words:1.2) weighs its rows.
  • Flux is large: the transformer has 12 billion weights (about 6.5 GB in Q4_K, 12 GB in Q8_0, 24 GB in F16) and T5 XXL 4.7 billion (about 5 GB in Q8_0), and it is slow on a CPU: start with FLUX.1-schnell and smaller sizes (--width 512 --height 512).
  • Flux’s diffusers folders, Kontext (reference images), Fill / Depth / Canny and Flux 2 are not read.

Qwen-Image

Section titled “Qwen-Image”

Qwen-Image ships in three files, as Comfy-Org and stable-diffusion.cpp take it: the transformer (qwen_image_bf16.safetensors, or a GGUF of it such as city96’s Qwen_Image-Q4_K_M.gguf; ComfyUI’s FP8 file is not read), the VAE (qwen_image_vae.safetensors, Wan 2.1’s) and its text encoder, the language model Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct as a llama.cpp GGUF file (for example Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct-Q8_0.gguf; its mmproj file is not needed):

  • The prompt goes through the language model inside diffusers’ template (a system turn asking to describe the image, then the prompt as the user’s turn); the last hidden states of the user’s turn on are the text rows the transformer reads (at most 1024).
  • The transformer has double-stream blocks only, over the text rows and the 2 x 2 patches of the 16-channel latents, with rotary positions centred on the picture.
  • Qwen-Image samples with Euler along its flow-matching schedule (diffusers’ FlowMatchEulerDiscreteScheduler as the model configures it: shifted by the picture’s size, its last step at 0.02), 50 steps at guidance 4 by default (the guided prediction rescaled per patch to the length of the prompt’s, as diffusers does), 1024 x 1024, multiples of 16. Its own sizes are 1328 x 1328, 1664 x 928 and 928 x 1664.
  • --cfg 1 skips the negative prompt (half the work per step).
  • Qwen-Image is large: the transformer has 20 billion weights (about 12 GB in Q4_K_M, 21 GB in Q8_0) and the language model 7.6 billion (8 GB in Q8_0); on a CPU start with small pictures (--width 512 --height 512) and few steps.
  • Qwen-Image’s diffusers folders and Qwen-Image-Edit (reference images) are not read.

Z-Image, Lumina-Image 2.0 and Chroma

Section titled “Z-Image, Lumina-Image 2.0 and Chroma”

Three more families come in parts, as Comfy-Org and the GGUF files ship them (built on tiny synthetic models, unverified on the real files: their memory and time are unmeasured). Each takes FLUX.1’s VAE (ae.safetensors; the kits take Comfy-Org’s copy, the same bytes as black-forest-labs’ without the gate) and one text encoder:

  • Z-Image Turbo (Tongyi-MAI, 6 billion weights, Apache 2.0): a single-stream NextDiT read by Qwen3-4B (the prompt in Qwen3’s chat template; the rows entering its last layer, as diffusers’ hidden_states[-2]), 9 steps without guidance by default (its card’s), Euler along a flow-matching schedule shifted by 3. Its card’s guidance_scale w is Inga’s --cfg 1+w (the Turbo model takes none).

  • Lumina-Image 2.0 (Alpha-VLLM, 2.6 billion weights, Apache 2.0; its text encoder under Google’s Gemma Terms of Use): the same NextDiT with grouped keys and values, read by Gemma-2-2B after diffusers’ system prompt, 50 steps at guidance 4 for the first quarter of them, renormed, as its card says (cfg_trunc_ratio=0.25, cfg_normalization=True); kit://lumina-image-2.

  • Chroma (lodestones’ Chroma1-HD, 8.9 billion weights, Apache 2.0): FLUX.1-schnell’s transformer whose modulations come from one small network, read by T5 XXL alone (no --clip-l), 40 steps at guidance 3 with a negative prompt (its card’s):

  • The three edit pictures (Transform, Touch up, Extend) through FLUX.1’s VAE encoder.

  • Their diffusers folders, Z-Image Omni and Z-Image-Edit are not read.

Sana

Section titled “Sana”

Sana (NVIDIA, Apache 2.0; its text encoder under Google’s Gemma Terms of Use), in diffusers’ files (built on tiny synthetic models, unverified on the real files: its memory and time are unmeasured). Its DiT has linear attention (ReLU, no softmax) and a convolution in its feed-forward; its autoencoder, DC-AE, makes a 32 x 32 block of pixels from each latent pixel, so a 1024 x 1024 picture is only 32 x 32 tokens for the transformer:

  • Gemma-2-2B-it reads the prompt after Sana’s complex human instruction (as diffusers’ SanaPipeline, the prompt lower-cased); the negative prompt is read without it.
  • 20 steps at guidance 4.5 by default, 1024 x 1024, sides multiples of 32; it samples with its own scheduler (flow DPM-Solver++ of order 2 shifted by 3, its scheduler_config.json), so --sampler stays at its default.
  • A diffusers folder (Efficient-Large-Model/Sana_600M_1024px_diffusers and the like) is read whole, with --llm for Gemma-2-2B-it. Sana 1.5 loads too.
  • On the CPU only, and it creates only (DC-AE’s encoder is not in Inga). Sana Sprint and the 2K / 4K models are not read.

4. Draw from the browser or a client

Section titled “4. Draw from the browser or a client”

Open http://127.0.0.1:8080/: the chat page sees an image model and turns into its image mode (the prompt box draws; size, steps, guidance, seed and the negative prompt are in the settings panel; the pictures stay in the browser). The menu’s Images page draws too, with Create, Transform (image to image), Inpaint, Extend and Upscale (edit-images.md), the aspect ratio and the resolution, and the pictures’ Download (see chat-in-the-browser.md); the Models page’s Images section downloads SD-Turbo, SDXL-Turbo, FLUX.1 schnell or Qwen-Image with all their files and gives the command that serves each.

Clients send OpenAI’s request; the images come back as base64 PNG (b64_json):

Besides OpenAI’s prompt, n (up to 4, seeds seed, seed + 1, …), size (WIDTHxHEIGHT) and response_format (b64_json), the server reads negative_prompt, steps, cfg_scale (or guidance_scale), seed and sampler (euler_a, euler). One image is drawn at a time; a second request waits for the first.

One server for chat and images

Section titled “One server for chat and images”

inga serve IMAGE_MODEL starts the same server as inga serve CHAT_MODEL, with the image model in its image slot: a chat model loads beside it from the web UI or POST /api/inga/load, with no restart. A chat server loads an image model the same way: choose one on the web UI’s Images page (it loads there, with its progress), POST /api/inga/media/load {"kind": "image", "model": "hf://stabilityai/sd-turbo/sd_turbo.safetensors"}, or name its reference (a path, hf://, kit://flux-schnell) as the request’s model; another name (OpenAI’s dall-e-2) is drawn by the model chosen. The choice is kept as image_model in /api/inga/settings. GET /api/inga/media says what the slot holds: empty, loading with the load’s progress, ready, or failed with the reason.

One memory budget holds both: before an image model loads, while the RAM left (under --mem-limit, else what the system says is available) is less than its files and a 512 x 512 drawing take, the least recently used idle chat model is unloaded (its prompt caches kept, as --vram-evict keeps them), and said on stderr, in the load’s progress, in /health and on the Images page; it loads again at its next request, and then an idle image model gives way the same way when the two do not fit. A model kept forever (keep_alive: -1) or answering a request is never unloaded. An idle image model is unloaded as the chat models are (--idle-unload, a request’s keep_alive).

An image is a memory-heavy job: it waits while another Inga of the same user loads a model and the two together would exceed the RAM available (the images and loads queue in ~/.cache/inga/jobs/, first in, first out). The web UI then says queued: waiting for load …, the console prints why, and GET /api/inga/jobs lists the queue.

LoRA: a style or a subject added to the model

Section titled “LoRA: a style or a subject added to the model”

A LoRA is a small file (a few MB to a few hundred) trained to add a style, a subject or a character to a base model. Inga applies LoRAs to Stable Diffusion 1.x / 2.x and SDXL (SD-Turbo, SDXL-Turbo, the SDXL and 1.5 finetunes included), on the UNet and the CLIP text encoders, from .safetensors files in either common layout: kohya’s (A1111, ComfyUI: lora_unet_…, lora_te1_… with lora_down / lora_up / alpha) and diffusers’ / PEFT’s (unet.… with lora_A / lora_B). Also to Qwen-Image‘s transformer, its layers under diffusers’ names (transformer_blocks.N.attn.to_q, with or without transformer. before them, or kohya’s lora_unet_transformer_blocks_…), as lightx2v’s Qwen-Image-Lightning files name them. Flux and SD 3 LoRAs are refused, and so are LoHa, LoKr and DoRA files.

Qwen-Image Lightning: lightx2v’s distillation LoRAs draw Qwen-Image in 8 or 4 steps without guidance. A LoRA whose file is named *lightning*Nsteps* sets the default steps to N without guidance, and on Qwen-Image the schedule it was distilled with (its card’s scheduler_config: shift 3 at every size, no terminal stretch), said on its line; kit://qwen-image-lightning is Qwen-Image’s three files and the 8-step V2.0 LoRA (Apache 2.0). On the quantized transformer of the kit the LoRA is added at run time, on the CPU (unmeasured, and unverified on the real files):

--lora FILE[:WEIGHT] takes a path or an hf://owner/repo/file.safetensors reference (downloaded into the Hugging Face cache, as models are); the weight multiplies the file’s alpha / rank (1 is the strength it was trained at, 0 leaves the model as it is, from -8 to 8). Several LoRAs add up. Inga says what each did, in a line of this shape (N, M and L are the file’s counts):

The names the model does not have are counted, not refused (N of M tensors applied, with a few of them named); a LoRA made for another base is refused with why, from its names (an SDXL LoRA has a second text encoder) or its shapes (“…attn2.to_k is 640 x 768 in the LoRA, the model’s is 640 x 2048”). Many LoRAs need their trigger words in the prompt (pixel art above): their page says which.

How it is applied: a checkpoint in F16 or F32 (the .safetensors files) gets each LoRA merged into its weights as they load (W + weight · alpha/rank · B · A), so the drawing runs at its usual speed; a quantized GGUF checkpoint (Q8_0, Q4_K) keeps its weights and adds the LoRA’s term at every step instead (x W + scale · (x A) B, as the language models’ LoRAs do), on the CPU only. Neither cost is measured.

Finding LoRAs

Section titled “Finding LoRAs”

The Hugging Face Hub is the directory: its models tagged lora whose card names the base model, which Inga searches for the loaded model’s base. On the web UI’s Images page, open LoRA under the settings: search (for example watercolor), add a file, set its weight with the slider (0 to 2, the value shown), remove it; the line under the picture says how many tensors each applied. Changing the LoRAs or a weight loads the model again before the next picture. The Models page’s Images section has the quick choice Takes LoRAs: the models they apply to.

From a client:

base is sd15, sd2 or sdxl (left out: the loaded model’s); the answer lists the repositories, most downloaded first, with their .safetensors files (ref), licence and trigger words; those the Hub marks not-for-all-audiences are left out and counted. A drawing’s loras (also a form field of /v1/images/edits, as JSON text) is the set for that picture: a request without it draws without LoRAs, and a set or weight other than the last loads the model again (its files are mapped, so mostly from the page cache). The answer’s inga.loras says what each did.

Each LoRA has its own licence, which Inga cannot check: read it on the file’s page. Civitai, the other large directory, is not searched (its files come with mixed licences and much adult content); it may become a source later.

On an NVIDIA GPU (experimental)

Section titled “On an NVIDIA GPU (experimental)”

The UNet and the VAE of Stable Diffusion 1.x, 2.x, SD-Turbo, SDXL and SDXL-Turbo can run on a CUDA GPU; the text encoders stay on the CPU. It is off until it is measured on an A100: turn it on with --set debug.image_gpu=true (or debug.image_gpu = true in inga.toml).

Before the prompt is encoded, inga image (and inga serve, and the web UI’s Images page) says where each part runs and why, for example:

  • The UNet is placed first (it runs every step), then the VAE, each where its weights and activations (estimated from the picture’s size) fit in the GPU’s free memory; a part that does not fit runs on the CPU.
  • F32 files (SD 1.5, SD-Turbo) go to the GPU in F16, half the memory; the file and the CPU keep F32. The VAE computes in f32 on the GPU too.
  • The transformer of Flux, SD 3 and Qwen-Image goes to the GPU the same way (the transformer (22.17 GiB, F16 from the file's BF16): BF16 weights are uploaded in F16), with Flux’s and SD 3’s VAE; Qwen-Image’s Wan VAE and every text encoder stay on the CPU. A VAE on a GPU whose memory does not hold the whole picture decodes it by tiles, said on the line (the VAE decodes 1024 x 1024 by tiles of 512 pixels). FLUX.1-schnell Q4_K_S has drawn on a 16 GB Tesla T4 (Colab, exp/0.44 d21e964, issue #1049): 512 x 512 at 7.05 s a step with a peak of 9232 MiB, and 1024 x 1024 (its time not kept: the runtime stopped). The PSNR against the CPU’s picture and the other models are unmeasured: the setting stays off by default. Vulkan, HIP and Metal draw on the CPU, said so on the line.
  • In inga serve, the image slot places the model the same way, on the server’s --device. The memory budget counts the whole of its files before it loads (where the parts go is decided by the load), then only what stays in RAM: GET /api/inga/media’s bytes leaves out the parts on the GPU, and its gpu_bytes says what they take there. The GPU’s own memory is not part of that budget: the plan fits the parts in the free memory the driver reports when the model loads, after what the chat models hold.
  • The picture on the GPU is close to the CPU’s, not the same bytes: how close (its PSNR) is measured on the A100 with the release, and not verified before.

How it works

Section titled “How it works”
  • The prompt is split into CLIP tokens (the vocabulary ships inside inga), 77 per chunk; the text encoder (CLIP ViT-L/14 for 1.x, OpenCLIP ViT-H/14’s penultimate layer for 2.x) turns each chunk into 77 rows the UNet reads through cross-attention. SDXL has two: the penultimate layers of CLIP ViT-L/14 and OpenCLIP ViT-bigG/14 side by side (2048 values per row), and a vector added to the UNet’s timestep embedding: bigG’s pooled output of the prompt, then the original size, the crop (0, 0) and the target size (the picture’s). Without a negative prompt, SDXL’s unconditioned rows are zeros (as in stable-diffusion.cpp and ComfyUI).
  • The noise comes from the seed through the same Philox generator as PyTorch on CUDA (stable-diffusion.cpp’s default), in a 64 x 64 x 4 latent for a 512 x 512 picture.
  • Each step runs the UNet once (twice with guidance: once for the prompt, once for the negative prompt, as one batch); the Euler samplers walk the 1000-step DDPM schedule from sigma 14.6 down to 0. A Stable Diffusion 2.x checkpoint trained on velocities (the 768 models) is recognized, as stable-diffusion.cpp does.
  • Flux reads T5’s rows and CLIP-L’s pooled output; its latents (16 channels) are cut into 2 x 2 patches, mixed with the text rows by double-stream then single-stream transformer blocks with rotary positions, each modulated by the timestep, the guidance and the pooled text; Euler steps follow Flux’s schedule, shifted by the picture’s size, from sigma 1 down to 0.
  • Qwen-Image reads the language model’s last hidden states, normed and projected; its latents are Wan 2.1’s (16 channels, un-normalized by a mean and a deviation per channel before the VAE), whose causal 3D decoder runs on one frame: each 3 x 3 x 3 convolution is its last slice, with RMS norms over the channels.
  • The VAE decoder turns the latents into pixels. Convolutions, attention and linear layers are matrix products in f32 on every core, with AVX2 / AVX-512 kernels; the results are checked against stable-diffusion.cpp on tiny checkpoints (tools/diffusion-oracle.md).

Measured on a 4-core Xeon (AVX-512) with an SD-Turbo-sized checkpoint, 512 x 512, one step: 0.5 s for the prompt, 5 s for the UNet, 9.5 s for the VAE decoder (about 15 s in all; stable-diffusion.cpp took 30 s on the same machine). Each extra step adds one UNet pass (two with guidance).

Troubleshooting

Section titled “Troubleshooting”
  • not an image model: the file has no UNet (model.diffusion_model.*), for example a VAE or a text encoder alone, or a language model.
  • `flux2` is Flux 2: Flux 2 and the SDXL refiner do not run; take a single-file Flux.1 or SDXL checkpoint.
  • a Qwen-Image model without its text encoder: give Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct with --llm FILE; Qwen-Image reads text rows of 3584 values: the language model given is another one.
  • an SDXL UNet without SDXL's two text encoders: the SDXL refiner (one text encoder, an aesthetic score) and SDXL split into parts (a ComfyUI-GGUF UNet file, a diffusers folder) are not read: use the base checkpoint’s single file.
  • the width must be a multiple of 64: the UNet halves the latents three times (twice for SDXL: multiples of 32); use 512, 576, 640, 768, …
  • ... is an image model: draw with inga image: inga run and the other commands are for language models.
  • The picture is noise or gray: give an SD-Turbo file 1 to 4 steps with --cfg 1, and other checkpoints 20 or more steps with --cfg around 7.
