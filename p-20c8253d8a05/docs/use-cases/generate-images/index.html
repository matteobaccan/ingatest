Generate images from text

Supported since: 0.20.0 (SDXL and SDXL-Turbo since 0.24.0; models in parts, diffusers folders, SD 3.x and Flux.1 since 0.25.0; Qwen-Image since 0.28.0)

What you get

Section titled “What you get”

A picture from a sentence, on the CPU: inga image writes a PNG file with Stable Diffusion 1.x, 2.x, SDXL, SD-Turbo, SDXL-Turbo, SD 3.x, Flux.1 (dev, schnell) or Qwen-Image, and inga serve offers the same model to the browser (an image mode in the chat page) and to OpenAI clients (/v1/images/generations). For the same checkpoint, prompt, size, seed and settings you get the image stable-diffusion.cpp and the AUTOMATIC1111 web UI give. To redraw a picture of yours, or only a part of it, see edit-images.md.

Requirements

Section titled “Requirements”
  • Windows, Linux or macOS; any x86-64 or ARM64 CPU (AVX2 or AVX-512 make it much faster).
  • A checkpoint in one file: a .safetensors file in the original layout (what the AUTOMATIC1111 web UI and ComfyUI load) or a GGUF file converted by stable-diffusion.cpp; or a model in parts (a ComfyUI-GGUF UNet with its VAE and CLIP files, a diffusers folder; see “Models in parts” below). Stable Diffusion 2.x and SD-Turbo have 1.3 billion weights (2.6 GB in F16, twice that in F32), Stable Diffusion 1.x 1.1 billion; drawing 512 x 512 pictures takes about the file’s size plus 2 to 3 GB of free RAM. SDXL and SDXL-Turbo have 3.5 billion weights (6.9 GB in F16); count the file’s size plus 4 to 8 GB of free RAM for 1024 x 1024 pictures.
  • Network access for the download (see run-a-model-from-hugging-face.md); some repositories ask you to accept their licence on huggingface.co first, then to set HF_TOKEN.

1. Get Inga

Section titled “1. Get Inga”

Download the archive for your system from the Releases page and put inga on your PATH (details); inga --version checks it. This guide needs 0.20.0 or later.

2. Get a model

Section titled “2. Get a model”

SD-Turbo draws a usable picture in one step, the fastest choice on a CPU. Name the checkpoint file of its repository:

Other checkpoints work the same way, for example a Stable Diffusion 1.5 file (hf://<org>/<repo>/<file>.safetensors), SDXL (hf://stabilityai/stable-diffusion-xl-base-1.0/sd_xl_base_1.0.safetensors) and SDXL-Turbo (hf://stabilityai/sdxl-turbo/sd_xl_turbo_1.0_fp16.safetensors), or a GGUF conversion (hf://<org>/<repo>:Q8_0 or /<file>.gguf): quantized GGUF files are smaller and their matrices run on the quantized kernels of the language models. A checkpoint already on disk is used by its path. Models in parts are read too: see “Models in parts” below.

3. Draw

Section titled “3. Draw”

A file whose name contains turbo draws in 1 step without guidance; other checkpoints take 20 steps with a guidance scale of 7, as stable-diffusion.cpp does. Pictures are 512 x 512 by default, 1024 x 1024 for SDXL (its training size; SDXL-Turbo’s is 512 x 512). To repeat an image, give its seed with --seed; to change it, change the seed or the prompt:

On Windows (PowerShell) put the command on one line, or end the lines with a backtick instead of \.

The prompt takes the AUTOMATIC1111 syntax: (words) weighs them up by 1.1, [words] down, (words:1.5) by 1.5, and BREAK starts a new chunk of 75 tokens (longer prompts are cut into chunks by themselves).

Models in parts

Section titled “Models in parts”

Some repositories ship the UNet alone (ComfyUI-GGUF’s quantized UNets) and leave the VAE and the text encoders to files of their own, as stable-diffusion.cpp’s --diffusion-model, --vae, --clip_l and --clip_g take them. Name each part:

Inga first says which file gives which part (--vae: sdxl_vae.safetensors, …).

  • The UNet may also be the model itself (inga image unet.gguf --vae ... --clip-l ...). Stable Diffusion 1.x / 2.x take --clip-l only (their one text encoder); SDXL takes both.
  • The VAE given with --vae replaces the one of a whole checkpoint, for instance SDXL’s fp16-fix VAE: inga image sd_xl_base_1.0.safetensors --vae sdxl_vae.safetensors -p ....
  • Each part may be in the original names, in diffusers’ (down_blocks.*, decoder.up_blocks.*) or, for the text encoders, in Hugging Face’s (text_model.*, ComfyUI’s clip_l.safetensors / clip_g.safetensors).
  • A diffusers folder (model_index.json with unet/, vae/, text_encoder/ and, for SDXL, text_encoder_2/) is one model: inga image ./stable-diffusion-xl-base-1.0 -p .... The *.fp16.safetensors files are taken when the folder has them.
  • A part that is missing is named in the error, with the option that gives it.
  • inga serve takes the same options (inga serve --diffusion-model unet.gguf --vae ... --clip-l ... --clip-g ...).

Stable Diffusion 3.x

Section titled “Stable Diffusion 3.x”

SD 3 Medium and SD 3.5 (Large, Large Turbo, Medium) come as one file with the MMDiT and the VAE; their three text encoders are inside it (sd3_medium_incl_clips_t5xxlfp16. safetensors) or in files of their own (clip_l.safetensors, clip_g.safetensors, t5xxl_fp16.safetensors):

  • SD 3.x samples with Euler along its flow schedule (shift 3), 28 steps at guidance 4.5 by default (a file named *turbo*, such as SD 3.5 Large Turbo: 4 steps without guidance), 1024 x 1024, multiples of 16 up to the model’s position table (192 patches a side: 3072 pixels for SD 3 Medium).
  • The prompt goes to the three encoders, 77 tokens per chunk; the weighted syntax works for each of them.
  • Diffusers folders of SD 3 and SD 3.5’s skip-layer guidance are not read.

Flux

Section titled “Flux”

Flux.1 ships in four files: the transformer (flux1-dev.safetensors, flux1-schnell.safetensors, or a GGUF of it such as city96’s flux1-dev-Q4_K_S.gguf), the VAE (ae.safetensors), CLIP-L (clip_l.safetensors) and T5 v1.1 XXL (t5xxl_fp16.safetensors, or a GGUF of the encoder):

  • Flux samples with Euler along its shifted schedule, without classifier-free guidance (--cfg 1): FLUX.1-dev takes its guidance as an input instead (--guidance, 3.5 by default) and 20 steps; FLUX.1-schnell 4 steps. Pictures are 1024 x 1024 by default, multiples of 16.
  • --cfg above 1 also runs the negative prompt (twice the work per step).
  • The T5 tokenizer’s vocabulary ships inside inga; T5 reads the prompt in chunks of 256 tokens, and the weighted syntax (words:1.2) weighs its rows.
  • Flux is large: the transformer has 12 billion weights (about 6.5 GB in Q4_K, 12 GB in Q8_0, 24 GB in F16) and T5 XXL 4.7 billion (about 5 GB in Q8_0), and it is slow on a CPU: start with FLUX.1-schnell and smaller sizes (--width 512 --height 512).
  • Flux’s diffusers folders, Kontext (reference images), Fill / Depth / Canny and Flux 2 are not read.

Qwen-Image

Section titled “Qwen-Image”

Qwen-Image ships in three files, as Comfy-Org and stable-diffusion.cpp take it: the transformer (qwen_image_bf16.safetensors, or a GGUF of it such as city96’s Qwen_Image-Q4_K_M.gguf; ComfyUI’s FP8 file is not read yet), the VAE (qwen_image_vae.safetensors, Wan 2.1’s) and its text encoder, the language model Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct as a llama.cpp GGUF file (for example Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct-Q8_0.gguf; its mmproj file is not needed):

  • The prompt goes through the language model inside diffusers’ template (a system turn asking to describe the image, then the prompt as the user’s turn); the last hidden states of the user’s turn on are the text rows the transformer reads (at most 1024).
  • The transformer has double-stream blocks only, over the text rows and the 2 x 2 patches of the 16-channel latents, with rotary positions centred on the picture.
  • Qwen-Image samples with Euler along its flow-matching schedule (diffusers’ FlowMatchEulerDiscreteScheduler as the model configures it: shifted by the picture’s size, its last step at 0.02), 50 steps at guidance 4 by default (the guided prediction rescaled per patch to the length of the prompt’s, as diffusers does), 1024 x 1024, multiples of 16. Its own sizes are 1328 x 1328, 1664 x 928 and 928 x 1664.
  • --cfg 1 skips the negative prompt (half the work per step).
  • Qwen-Image is large: the transformer has 20 billion weights (about 12 GB in Q4_K_M, 21 GB in Q8_0) and the language model 7.6 billion (8 GB in Q8_0); on a CPU start with small pictures (--width 512 --height 512) and few steps.
  • Qwen-Image’s diffusers folders and Qwen-Image-Edit (reference images) are not read.

4. Draw from the browser or a client

Section titled “4. Draw from the browser or a client”

Open http://127.0.0.1:8080/: the chat page sees an image model and turns into its image mode (the prompt box draws; size, steps, guidance, seed and the negative prompt are in the settings panel; the pictures stay in the browser).

Clients send OpenAI’s request; the images come back as base64 PNG (b64_json):

Besides OpenAI’s prompt, n (up to 4, seeds seed, seed + 1, …), size (WIDTHxHEIGHT) and response_format (b64_json), the server reads negative_prompt, steps, cfg_scale (or guidance_scale), seed and sampler (euler_a, euler). One image is drawn at a time; an image model is served alone.

Since 0.27.0 an image is a memory-heavy job: it waits while another Inga of the same user loads a model and the two together would exceed the RAM available (the images and loads queue in ~/.cache/inga/jobs/, first in, first out). The web UI then says queued: waiting for load …, the console prints why, and GET /api/inga/jobs lists the queue.

Options worth knowing

Section titled “Options worth knowing”
Option Meaning
-p, --prompt TEXT What to draw
--negative TEXT What to steer away from (used when --cfg is not 1)
-o, --output FILE The PNG file (default image.png)
--steps N Sampling steps (20; 1 for *turbo* files; SD-Turbo: 1 to 4)
--cfg X Classifier-free guidance scale (7; 1 for *turbo*: no negative prompt, one UNet pass per step)
--width N, --height N Size in pixels, multiples of 64 (32 for SDXL), up to 2048 (512; 1024 for SDXL, 512 for *turbo* SDXL files)
--sampler S euler_a (Euler ancestral, the default) or euler (the default and only one for SD 3.x, Flux and Qwen-Image)
--seed N The noise’s seed (default: from the clock)
--diffusion-model F The UNet in a file of its own (when the model is not given or holds no UNet)
--vae F The VAE in a file of its own (also replaces a checkpoint’s)
--clip-l F, --clip-g F CLIP ViT-L/14 (the text encoder of 1.x / 2.x, SDXL’s first, Flux’s pooled text) and OpenCLIP ViT-bigG/14 (SDXL’s second)
--t5xxl F T5 v1.1 XXL (Flux’s and SD 3’s text rows)
--llm F A language model as text encoder: Qwen-Image’s Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct (a GGUF file)
--guidance X FLUX.1-dev’s distilled guidance (3.5)
-t, --threads N CPU threads (all logical CPUs)

How it works

Section titled “How it works”
  • The prompt is split into CLIP tokens (the vocabulary ships inside inga), 77 per chunk; the text encoder (CLIP ViT-L/14 for 1.x, OpenCLIP ViT-H/14’s penultimate layer for 2.x) turns each chunk into 77 rows the UNet reads through cross-attention. SDXL has two: the penultimate layers of CLIP ViT-L/14 and OpenCLIP ViT-bigG/14 side by side (2048 values per row), and a vector added to the UNet’s timestep embedding: bigG’s pooled output of the prompt, then the original size, the crop (0, 0) and the target size (the picture’s). Without a negative prompt, SDXL’s unconditioned rows are zeros (as in stable-diffusion.cpp and ComfyUI).
  • The noise comes from the seed through the same Philox generator as PyTorch on CUDA (stable-diffusion.cpp’s default), in a 64 x 64 x 4 latent for a 512 x 512 picture.
  • Each step runs the UNet once (twice with guidance: once for the prompt, once for the negative prompt, as one batch); the Euler samplers walk the 1000-step DDPM schedule from sigma 14.6 down to 0. A Stable Diffusion 2.x checkpoint trained on velocities (the 768 models) is recognized, as stable-diffusion.cpp does.
  • Flux reads T5’s rows and CLIP-L’s pooled output; its latents (16 channels) are cut into 2 x 2 patches, mixed with the text rows by double-stream then single-stream transformer blocks with rotary positions, each modulated by the timestep, the guidance and the pooled text; Euler steps follow Flux’s schedule, shifted by the picture’s size, from sigma 1 down to 0.
  • Qwen-Image reads the language model’s last hidden states, normed and projected; its latents are Wan 2.1’s (16 channels, un-normalized by a mean and a deviation per channel before the VAE), whose causal 3D decoder runs on one frame: each 3 x 3 x 3 convolution is its last slice, with RMS norms over the channels.
  • The VAE decoder turns the latents into pixels. Convolutions, attention and linear layers are matrix products in f32 on every core, with AVX2 / AVX-512 kernels; the results are checked against stable-diffusion.cpp on tiny checkpoints (tools/diffusion-oracle.md).

Measured on a 4-core Xeon (AVX-512) with an SD-Turbo-sized checkpoint, 512 x 512, one step: 0.5 s for the prompt, 5 s for the UNet, 9.5 s for the VAE decoder (about 15 s in all; stable-diffusion.cpp took 30 s on the same machine). Each extra step adds one UNet pass (two with guidance).

Troubleshooting

Section titled “Troubleshooting”
  • not an image model: the file has no UNet (model.diffusion_model.*), for example a VAE or a text encoder alone, or a language model.
  • `flux2` is Flux 2: Flux 2 and the SDXL refiner come in 0.34.0 (TODO.md).
  • a Qwen-Image model without its text encoder: give Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct with --llm FILE; Qwen-Image reads text rows of 3584 values: the language model given is another one.
  • an SDXL UNet without SDXL's two text encoders: the SDXL refiner (one text encoder, an aesthetic score) and SDXL split into parts (a ComfyUI-GGUF UNet file, a diffusers folder) are not read: use the base checkpoint’s single file.
  • the width must be a multiple of 64: the UNet halves the latents three times (twice for SDXL: multiples of 32); use 512, 576, 640, 768, …
  • ... is an image model: draw with inga image: inga run and the other commands are for language models.
  • The picture is noise or gray: give an SD-Turbo file 1 to 4 steps with --cfg 1, and other checkpoints 20 or more steps with --cfg around 7.
