Customize a model: remove its refusals, with its licence read first

What you get

Section titled “What you get”

You start from a model on your computer and change it: Inga explains every step and every change in plain words (what it does, why, the pros, the cons, the cost, what it changes, how to undo it), reads the model’s licence and says whether it allows the change, asks for your consent, makes the change, measures the result beside the original and writes it as a new file next to it. The original is never changed: to undo, delete the new file.

The changes:

  • Remove refusals (abliterate): for a dense model (Llama, Qwen, Mistral, Phi and the like) that refuses ordinary requests too often. Inga finds, layer by layer, the direction the model takes when it refuses (from two lists of prompts: JBB-Behaviors’ harmful behaviours, MIT, and oasst1’s first user turns, Apache-2.0) and takes it out of the attention’s output and the MLP’s down projection. The model then loses its safeguards: the change and its use are your responsibility. Inga stops where the licence forbids it: the Llama and Gemma use policies forbid taking away a model’s safeguards.
  • Copy, unchanged (identity): the same steps without a change, to see them, or to write the model in another quantization with its record.

Requirements

Section titled “Requirements”
  • Windows, Linux or macOS; the change and its measures run on the CPU.
  • Disk for one more model of the original’s size (each type you pick is one more file).
  • For Remove refusals, the two datasets (23 KB and 34 MB): Inga says their size and licence and fetches them only with your go, then keeps them in ~/.cache/inga/customize/datasets.

1. Get Inga

Section titled “1. Get Inga”

Download the archive for your OS from the Releases page, extract it, and put the inga executable on your PATH; check it with inga --version.

2. In the web UI

Section titled “2. In the web UI”

inga serve, then Models → Customize: pick the model, the change (its card opens with Technical detail), read the licence’s verdict, tick the consent, choose the quantization of the result and press Customize. The progress says each phase; at the end the table shows the original and the result side by side (the first token’s KL divergence, the perplexity on a short text, the size, the speed when asked), the comparison in words (“answers 2 % less precise on this text, the same size”), the refusals counted before and after, and the files written.

3. On the command line

Section titled “3. On the command line”

The result is Qwen3-0.6B-abliterate-Q8_0.gguf beside the original, with Qwen3-0.6B-abliterate-Q8_0.gguf.customize.json: the reproduce file (the source’s SHA-256, the change, its settings and parameters, the datasets with their SHA-256, the type, the measures, your consent with its time). inga customize --reproduce FILE makes the same output again and says whether its bytes are the record’s.

A model with no licence in its file, its model card or its Ollama licence layer is not changed until you state one with --licence ID (you take the responsibility; it is recorded). From an F16 or BF16 copy of the model beside the file you picked, Inga reads the weights at full precision, so the result is quantized once; from a quantized source it says that the precision is lost twice and asks before it goes on.

Settings of Remove refusals

Section titled “Settings of Remove refusals”

--param ID=VALUE (the web UI shows the same fields): prompts (100 of each list), tokens (100, the tokens of a reply read to see whether it refuses), system (a system prompt), projected (on: the part of the direction ordinary answers share is left alone), norm (on: only the weights’ direction changes, not their size), direction (the layer of the direction; -1: the one where the two lists differ the most), attn and mlp (the strength on each part; -1: 1 and 0.5).

Keep only the change, as an adapter

Section titled “Keep only the change, as an adapter”

--adapter only (the page’s “Also as an adapter”) writes the change of Remove refusals as a small file, Qwen3-0.6B-abliterate-lora.gguf, in place of a second model: load it with --lora on the original (inga run Qwen3-0.6B-Q8_0.gguf --lora Qwen3-0.6B-abliterate-lora.gguf, llama.cpp too). --adapter beside writes it beside the changed model.

Steer the original instead, no weight changed

Section titled “Steer the original instead, no weight changed”

Remove refusals also writes the refusal direction it found beside the model, as Qwen3-0.6B-abliterate-steer.gguf (llama.cpp’s control-vector format, a few MB; one already there is kept unless --force). It changes nothing in the model: --steer adds the direction to the model’s activations while it answers, at the strength you give, so the same file runs steered or not from one chat to the next.

The start says what it does (note: steering by FILE at strength -1 after N layers, A to B (added to the activations, the weights untouched)), and each answer of inga serve carries it as inga_steering. It runs on the CPU and on the GPU’s layers. Steering per conversation is in Steer a model with control vectors.

Search the best settings

Section titled “Search the best settings”

With --search (the page’s “Search the best settings”) Inga tries many settings of Remove refusals (100 by default, --trials N; the first 30 at random, then where the best ones were) and measures each: how many prompts it still refuses and how far its answers move from the original’s. It then lists the best trade-offs, none better than another on both counts, the suggested one marked, and you pick one (--pick N picks without asking). Each trial is a whole measure, so a search takes as many times longer as it has trials. The seed (--seed N), the list and your pick are written into the reproduce file.

How Inga decides

Section titled “How Inga decides”
  • The licence comes from the file’s general.license, else from the model card beside it in the Hugging Face cache (the license: of its README.md), else from an Ollama model’s licence layer, else the one you state; Inga says where it read it, and that its reading is not legal advice. Only files on your computer are read.
  • With --speed (the page’s box), the speeds of the original and of each file written are kept in the Speed page’s history and inga bench --history.
  • Refusals are counted by keywords in the reply (“sorry”, “I can’t”, “as an AI”, …): a disclaimer followed by an answer counts as a refusal, and a refusal worded otherwise is missed. The numbers before and after are shown so you can judge.
  • The measures run on a few prompts and a short text: a guide, not a benchmark (inga eval and inga perplexity measure more).
