Supported models

What Inga runs, by what the model files declare. To check a file before running it: inga info MODEL (any file), inga list (the Hugging Face cache), inga scan DIR (a folder tree) and inga search WORDS --supported (Hugging Face) all say supported or why not; so do inga list --ollama and inga list --lmstudio for the models Ollama and LM Studio downloaded. How to use them: find-models-and-check-support.md.

Real file, in the tables below: ✅ and a date when a real, public file of that row was checked against its reference program on the maintainer’s runner (the same tokens as llama-tokenize, perplexity within 1 % of llama-perplexity, both on the CPU; real-world checks, group A, 2026-10-15); synthetic only when only synthetic files were compared with the reference. The nightly rotation (rotation.json, docs/maintainer-checklist.md) turns these into real checks a slot a night; its current state is the issue “Real-file checks — tracking”. Besides the rows marked here, real files checked on 2026-10-15: the Q8_0, Q4_K and Q6_K weights (inside those files), every GGUF vocabulary of llama.cpp’s models folder (BPE, SPM, WPM, UGM), every chat template of llama.cpp’s models/templates, and the expert streaming of --mem-limit (Qwen3-30B-A3B, the same text as all in RAM). Speech, embeddings, image generation and the Hugging Face formats are synthetic only.

A model that is not here? Ask for it: the error message, the Hugging Face link and what you want to do with it are enough.

Diffusion language models

Section titled “Diffusion language models”

The answer is denoised from masks in a few steps, many tokens at once, instead of being written token by token; their attention is not causal. inga run and inga serve use them as any chat model (the answer comes when its blocks are final, not token by token); inga list, inga scan and inga info report them as supported (diffusion). See diffusion-language-models.md.

MLX weights Converted to
affine, 2, 3 or 4 bits (groups of 32, 64 or 128) Q4_K (Q8_0 for rows that are not whole 256-value blocks)

The affine weights with f16 or bf16 scales are not converted on the CPU and on Metal (--device cpu, metal, auto on a Mac): MLX kernels read them as stored (scale * q + bias, spec); the table above is what CUDA, Vulkan, --quantize and debug.mlx_native=false get.

Embedding and reranking models (encoders)

Section titled “Embedding and reranking models (encoders)”

The BERT family embeds texts (/v1/embeddings, Ollama’s /api/embed) and ranks them (/v1/rerank); these models do not chat or complete text, and say so. inga list, inga scan and inga info report them as supported (embeddings) or supported (reranker).

Tokenizers: WordPiece (tokenizer.json WordPiece, GGUF bert) and SentencePiece unigram (Unigram with its precompiled character map, GGUF t5). The pooling is the model’s: a sentence-transformers directory’s 1_Pooling/config.json (mean or CLS) and modules.json (embeddings scaled to unit length only with a Normalize module), a GGUF file’s pooling_type; inga serve --pooling overrides it. An embedding model asked to rerank scores the cosine similarity of the query’s and each document’s embeddings.

Gemma 4’s projector files hold the images’ and the audio’s encoders together: Inga reads both. So do Gemma 3n’s (gemma3nv and gemma3na): images and audio, computed as transformers’ Gemma3nAudioEncoder and laid out as its processor does (188 rows and the end-of-audio row per 30 seconds).

Phi-4-multimodal-instruct has no projector file: Inga reads its Hugging Face directory (microsoft/Phi-4-multimodal-instruct, or the layout of transformers’ conversion script with vision-lora/ and speech-lora/) whole. Images are cut as transformers’ processor cuts them (448-pixel crops, at most dynamic_hd = 36, plus a global view, through SigLIP, 2 x 2 average pooling and a two-layer GELU projector); speech goes through its conformer (80-bin log-mel features, 8x subsampling, windows of 500 rows). The vision LoRA adapter is applied while an image is in the prompt, the speech adapter while audio (and no image) is, neither for text (the server’s /lora-adapters lists them as adapters 0 and 1). Checked against transformers 5.19 on a synthetic model only (tools/phi4mm_oracle.py); a GGUF file converted as phi3 still reads text only.

Videos (--video, video_url and video parts) are read by the Qwen-VL projectors and SmolVLM’s: an animated GIF, MJPEG or a list of images, sampled as the models’ processors in transformers sample them (Qwen-VL: 2 frames a second, at most 768, by pairs through the patch convolution’s two temporal kernels; Qwen2-VL and Qwen2.5-VL place the pairs at M-RoPE time positions, Qwen3-VL behind their times as text; SmolVLM2: 1 frame a second, at most 64, each one tile behind Frame from MM:SS:). MP4 and WebM are not read: extract their frames as images.

Image generation (inga image)

Section titled “Image generation (inga image)”

Single-file checkpoints: a .safetensors file in the original layout (the one the AUTOMATIC1111 web UI and ComfyUI load: model.diffusion_model.*, first_stage_model.*, cond_stage_model.*; SDXL’s text encoders under conditioner.embedders.*) or a GGUF file as stable-diffusion.cpp converts one (the same names, SDXL’s encoders as cond_stage_model.* and cond_stage_model.1.*; weights in any llama.cpp type, the convolutions in F16 or F32). (see generate-images.md).

Models in parts, as stable-diffusion.cpp’s --diffusion-model, --vae, --clip_l and --clip_g take them: a UNet alone (--diffusion-model, or as the model: a ComfyUI-GGUF file, general.architecture sd1 / sdxl, quantized tensors ComfyUI-GGUF flattened read back in their shape; a .safetensors file in the original or the diffusers layout), the VAE (--vae, original or diffusers names; it also replaces a checkpoint’s, as SDXL’s fp16-fix VAE), CLIP ViT-L/14 (--clip-l) and OpenCLIP ViT-bigG/14 (--clip-g) in Hugging Face’s layout (ComfyUI’s clip_l.safetensors / clip_g.safetensors) or OpenCLIP’s. A diffusers folder (model_index.json, unet/, vae/, text_encoder/, text_encoder_2/; the *.fp16.safetensors variant when there is one, shards included) is read as one model. Flux comes in parts only: the transformer (as the model or --diffusion-model), --vae, --clip-l, --t5xxl; its diffusers folders, Kontext, Fill, Depth, Canny and Flux 2 are not read. Qwen-Image comes in parts too: the transformer (qwen_image_bf16.safetensors from Comfy-Org, not its FP8 file, or city96’s GGUF file, general.architecture qwen_image; as the model or --diffusion-model), its VAE (qwen_image_vae.safetensors, Wan 2.1’s, with --vae; Wan’s names or diffusers’) and Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct as text encoder (a llama.cpp GGUF file, with --llm); its diffusers folders and Qwen-Image-Edit are not read. Three more families in parts, each as ComfyUI’s and the GGUF files name it: Z-Image (Turbo) and Lumina-Image 2.0, whose NextDiT (x_embedder, noise_refiner, context_refiner, layers; a GGUF file’s general.architecture is lumina2 for both) is the model or --diffusion-model, FLUX.1’s ae.safetensors with --vae and the language model with --llm (Qwen3-4B for Z-Image, Gemma-2-2B for Lumina-Image 2.0, a llama.cpp GGUF file); and Chroma (Chroma1-HD, Chroma1-Base: FLUX.1-schnell’s transformer with a distilled_guidance_layer in place of its modulation layers, a GGUF file’s architecture flux) with --vae and --t5xxl, no CLIP. Their diffusers folders, Z-Image Omni and Z-Image-Edit are not read. Built on tiny synthetic models checked against references written from diffusers’ and ComfyUI’s code; unverified on the real files, their memory and time unmeasured (real-world checks, group E). Also Sana (NVIDIA’s linear-attention DiT: Sana 600M and 1600M at 512 and 1024 pixels, Sana 1.5 with its RMS-normed queries and keys) in diffusers’ layout: its transformer (transformer/diffusion_pytorch_model*.safetensors, SanaTransformer2DModel‘s names; its config.json beside it gives the cross attention’s heads, else 16 for a 1,152-wide model and 20 for a 2,240-wide one) as the model or --diffusion-model, DC-AE (vae/diffusion_pytorch_model.safetensors, AutoencoderDC) with --vae, Gemma-2-2B-it (a llama.cpp GGUF file) with --llm; or its diffusers folder (SanaPipeline) with --llm.
