Performance experiments

Every performance idea tried in Inga, whatever came of it. An idea that was measured and did not pay off is not deleted: it stays here as “set aside”, with its numbers, because it may work in another context (other hardware, models, batch sizes, context lengths). Before trying an idea, look for it here; after trying it, log it here.

The rule every performance pull request follows is in performance.md; the measured speeds of the released code are in performance.md.

Format

Section titled “Format”

One entry per idea, newest first within each verdict:

  • Source: the project, paper or link it comes from.
  • Date and Inga version it was measured with.
  • Measured on: the hardware and the model (file, type).
  • How: the command (inga bench ...), the same machine before and after.
  • Result: the numbers before and after.
  • Verdict: adopted, set aside or pending (not tried yet).
  • Why: what explains the result.
  • Could work when: the conditions under which it is worth trying again.

  • Source: FlashDecoding (Tri Dao, Daniel Haziza, Francisco Massa, Grigory Sizov, “Flash-Decoding for long-context inference”, 2023), llama.cpp’s fattn-vec kernels and their flash_attn_combine_results pass (parallel_blocks), vLLM’s paged attention v2 (partitions, then a reduce kernel); the A100 run of 0.38.0 (Qwen3-8B Q4_K_M tg128 143.0 tokens/s at depth 0, 116.8 at depth 256; Qwen3-30B-A3B 157.5 and 118.9; serving’s decode about 123, where llama.cpp b11489 loses much less with depth).

  • Date: 2026-10-24 · Inga version: 0.38.0+ (branch perf/cuda-flash-decode)

  • Idea: a decode step’s attention (attention_f16, attention_rows_f16) ran one block of 256 threads per (query head, row), which walked every position of the cache in tiles of 256, a thread computing a position’s whole dot product, then the values summed by 256 threads each over the tile’s positions: 32 blocks for Qwen3-8B on 108 multiprocessors, and the four query heads that share a key head reading it four times. The split-K kernel (flash_decode_f16_NP_GM in kernels/batch.cu) takes a block per (key head, group of up to 8 query heads, part, row); the parts split the positions the query sees (a share rounded up to 32), as many as give two blocks per multiprocessor, at most 64, fixed by the cache’s size so that a recorded graph replays at any position (a part beyond a row’s positions writes an empty state); each of the block’s four warps scores 32 positions at once (a lane reads a key whole, 16 bytes at a time, the queries from shared memory), keeps its own online softmax and sums the values with its lanes on the head’s values; the warps’ states are combined in the block, then flash_decode_reduce combines the parts (the sink joining the denominator). Heads of up to 256 values; heads of 512 and prompts keep the earlier kernels. Its room (8 MB) is made with the GPU, as a graph keeps its address. Second version (after the first A100 run): a part takes 128 positions at least (one tile per warp), the kernels read the positions on the device (fd_range) and run only the parts that hold some, a row of up to 128 positions runs in one block per key head that writes the output (the combining pass returns at once), and the combining pass reads the parts’ states with a thread each instead of one thread’s loops over them; the parts’ count does not depend on the rows (a session alone and in a batch, the same bits).   • Source: the A100 profile of issue #966 (Colab, nsys 2025.3.1, main 3efd3c1 against llama.cpp b11489, 2026-10-29): Qwen3-30B-A3B Q4_K_M generating at depths 64 to 192 spends 1229 µs a token in attention_f16 (48 launches, about 25.6 µs each) where llama.cpp’s flash_attn_ext_f16 takes about 9 µs a layer (its profile counts one token too few: 217 µs reported, about 435 real); every other kernel is within about 1.2x. gemma-3-4b-it’s flash_decode_f16_4_2 takes about 25.6 µs a layer at the same depths. llama.cpp runs its tensor cores’ kernel for the one-token decode too, a key head’s query heads packed in a tile (ncols2), the mask’s scan bounding the keys read.
  • Date: 2026-10-30 · Inga version: 0.38.0+ (branch perf/cuda-gqa-decode)
  • Idea: both kernels a short row runs on are a chain of dependent memory reads, not work: attention_f16 (one block per query head) has a thread read a key 2 bytes at a time and sum the values over the tile’s positions one after the other (14.7 µs plus 0.17 µs a position in the profile of the split-K entry); the split-K kernel gives a part at least 128 positions, so a row of up to 128 runs on one block per key head (4 for Qwen3-30B-A3B), each warp doing its tile’s 8 heads one after the other (48.5 µs). The three candidates, by their work: (a) the split-K kernel’s fixed cost is its parts of 128 positions, which its four warps of 32 need; smaller parts leave warps idle; (c) llama.cpp’s FATTN for decode needs a mask built on the device each step and the keys bounded by it inside the recorded graph, plus tiles of 8 columns that the port does not compile (64 on Ampere): new PTX for every head size and architecture; (b), chosen: short_decode_f16_NP_GM in kernels/batch.cu, a block of 4 warps per (key head, group of up to 8 query heads, part of 32 positions, row). Its threads issue every load of the part’s keys and values at once (16 bytes each) into shared memory, with the queries; a warp scores its heads (warp w: heads w and w + 4) with a lane per position, keeps the part’s softmax, then sums the values with its lanes on the head’s values. A row of up to 32 positions writes the output from its one block; a longer one writes its parts’ states as the split-K kernel does, the last part combining them (its room and counters, its layout). Qwen3-30B-A3B’s row of 256 positions runs on 32 blocks, of 64 on 8, with about three rounds of memory reads in the chain (the position and the queries, the keys and values, the parts). It takes the rows of up to 256 positions for every group (decode_min_len: Qwen3-8B’s 4 and Gemma 3’s 2 per key head too, where the split-K kernel’s fixed 18.5 µs began at 32), the split-K kernel the longer ones, each block reading its row’s length on the device; its parts are fixed by the cache, the window or the threshold, so a recorded graph replays at any length, a session alone and in a batch the same bits. Heads of 512, odd heads, an anchored window and HIP keep the earlier kernels.
  • Measured on: the A100-SXM4-40GB (Colab, issue #975: main 3efd3c1 against #972 e45fb50; issue #1000 part B: main c316205 with it on and off, ABBA), Qwen3-30B-A3B, Qwen3-8B and gemma-3-4b-it Q4_K_M; the Quadro P1000 (the maintainer, 2026-10-31, Qwen3-1.7B Q4_K_M). Here (no GPU): ptxas 12.4 for sm_80 gives 32 to 106 registers, no spills, 8.5 to 41.4 KB of static shared memory.
  • Result: the attention’s GPU tests pass on the A100 (12 passed, #975). tg128 on / off (#1000 B): Qwen3-8B 1.028 at depth 0, 1.039 at 32, 1.055 at 128, 0.995 at 256 and 0.994 at 1024; Qwen3-30B-A3B 0.990, 1.003, 1.094, 0.987, 0.987; gemma-3-4b-it 1.034, 1.041, 1.063, 0.995, 0.994 (#975 within 0.01 of these).   • Verdict: kept, on by default since 0.40.0 (#1026, the maintainer’s decision of 2026-10-31). Left: the loss of about 1 % from depth 256 on the A100 (the rows past the threshold) and the nsys kernel times with it on.

The routed experts kept in RAM through the GPU for prompts (MoE op offload)

Section titled “The routed experts kept in RAM through the GPU for prompts (MoE op offload)”
  • Source: llama.cpp’s op offload (ggml-org/llama.cpp#13386, #18535: from batches of GGML_OP_OFFLOAD_MIN_BATCH = 32 tokens, an operation whose weights are in host memory runs on the GPU after copying them); the entry above, whose next step this is (“measure again with the experts in RAM”, “a cost check that counts the bus”).
  • Date: 2026-11-01 · Inga version: 0.41.0 (branch exp/moe-op-offload, from exp/web-steps)
  • Idea: the maintainer’s P1000 (4 GB, PCIe measured at 5.1 GB/s) with Gemma 4 26B-A4B Q4_K_M keeps the routed experts of all 30 layers in RAM (14.1 GiB) and reads a prompt at about 26 tokens/s on the CPU (1010 tokens in 38.9 s). A forward pass takes up to 2048 prompt tokens, so each routed expert a step uses (all 128 of a layer, from a few hundred tokens) is copied once per layer and pass: about 470 MiB a layer, 0.1 s at 5.1 GB/s, against the CPU’s products of the same layer, about 0.5 ms a token (95 MFLOP a token at about 2·10^11 FLOP/s). It pays from a few hundred tokens a pass. With 25 MiB of VRAM left, the experts go one at a time through three rooms (an expert’s gate, up and down) and the activations of 128 rows; the rows are gathered and the outputs weighted on the CPU, so the per-token traffic is 2 · k · n_embd f32 (180 KB a token and layer) and the rooms do not grow with the pass.
  • Cost check: per layer and step, bytes / bus + flops · gpu < flops · cpu: the CPU’s seconds per operation measured on its own steps (the first eligible step stays on the CPU to measure it), the bus measured once (the best of three copies of a buffer held in RAM) then from the experts’ copies, the GPU’s seconds per operation (rows’ copies included) from its steps; running averages. Each change of decision is said on stderr with its numbers.
  • Measured on: not yet (no NVIDIA GPU where it was written; the CUDA code compiles, the layer loop is checked on HostOps: crates/inga-gpu/tests/offload.rs, Gemma 4, Qwen3 MoE Q8_0 and Qwen2 MoE synthetic models, the CPU’s logits within 1e-3 / 1e-4).
  • How: inga bench gemma-4-26B-A4B-it-Q4_K_M.gguf -p 512,1024 -n 32 -r 3 --set gpu.op_offload=off against =on and =auto (same load: --ab gpu.op_offload=off).
  • Result: pending.
  • Verdict: pending.
  • Could work better with: the copy of the next expert overlapped with the products of this one (pinned memory, a second stream, two rooms); the rows’ activations kept on the device for the whole pass when the memory allows (a third less traffic); Vulkan.

(a) GPU-resident sampling with the next step submitted ahead

Section titled “(a) GPU-resident sampling with the next step submitted ahead”
  • Source: uzu.
  • Date: 2026-10-09 · Inga version: 0.22.1-dev
  • Idea: sample on the GPU; submit step n+1 with the sampled token copied on the device, before the CPU reads it back, so the GPU never waits for the host.
  • Built (CUDA, first half): kernels/sampling.cu draws on the GPU what Sampler::sample_at draws on the CPU ((b)): sample_top_k_slices keeps the k best logits (by total_cmp, then lower id) of each slice of 4096 tokens — a thread keeps its own best and only the thread whose best was taken scans again — and sample_draw keeps the k best of those, then thread 0 computes the softmax, min-p, top-p and the Gumbel-max draw with Philox4x32-10. Only the token id (4 bytes) is copied back, against the whole row of logits (600 KB for Qwen3’s 151 936 tokens) and the CPU’s top-k before. Used when the output layer is on the GPU and the request is greedy or a temperature with top-k 1 to 128, top-p and min-p (SamplerCfg::device_sampling); everything else, and a driver refusing the kernels, samples on the CPU; debug.gpu_sampling=false turns it off. The two passes are checked step by step against the CPU’s sampler here (crates/inga-cuda/src/kernels.rs, 6 vocabularies up to 20 000 tokens with ties, NaN, infinities and signed zeros), and the whole path (GpuHead::sample, Model::forward_sampled, Session) on HostOps (crates/inga-gpu/tests/host.rs); the kernels themselves only on the GPU runner (crates/inga-cuda/tests/sampling.rs). The PTX was built with clang 18 and checked with ptxas 12.4 for sm_61 (no nvcc here): tools/build-ptx.sh rebuilds it with nvcc.
  • Built (Vulkan, 0.31.0): shaders/sample_prep.comp (the model’s logit scale and soft-cap, then the repetition, frequency and presence penalties of the recent tokens, in place), sample_top_k.comp (the k best of each slice of 4096 logits) and sample_draw.comp (the k best of those, sorted by rank, then the same draw as CUDA’s with Cephes’ expf, logf and tanhf to about one ulp, and umulExtended for Philox). The k best are found by a radix select (sample_select.glsl, a histogram of 8 bits at a time in shared memory, then the lowest ids among a tied key), whose steps do not grow with k: CUDA’s “each thread keeps its own best, k rounds” took 45.6 ms per draw at top-k 40 on 262,144 logits on lavapipe, the radix select 4.0 ms (1.2 ms greedy, a plain reduction). Checked on lavapipe against the CPU’s sampler (crates/inga-vulkan/tests/sampling.rs).
  • Not built yet: the next step submitted ahead. The token embeddings stay on the CPU (the layers take the hidden state from the host), so the sampled id must come back before step n+1 starts; the second half needs the embedding table on the GPU, a get_rows kernel reading the token id from the device buffer sample_draw writes, the decode step recorded on a stream (CUDA graphs, 2026-10-09-cuda-graphs-design.md) and the host reading token n while step n+1 runs, a stop (end of text, a stop sequence) costing one discarded step.
  • Measured on: not yet (no GPU here).
  • How: on the P1000 through the GPU runner, a model in INGA_MODELS with --gpu-layers all: inga run FILE -p "..." -n 256 --temp 0.8 --top-k 40 --seed 1 with debug.gpu_sampling=false and without (same binary; the answer is the same, the report line says N sampled on the GPU); SmolLM2-135M and Llama-3.2-1B / Qwen3-0.6B (large vocabularies) Q4_K_M / Q8_0; compare: base in gpu.yml does not show it (inga bench does not sample).
  • Result: pending on the P1000. Vulkan on lavapipe (2026-10-14, 4 cores shared with other jobs, load about 30; crates/inga-vulkan/tests/sampling.rs, device_sampling_speed: a synthetic Llama with 262,144 tokens and 64-wide embeddings, 48 tokens, three runs): bytes read back per token 1,048,832 with the logits, 260 sampled on the device (the token and the 256-byte hidden state); greedy 27.7 / 31.4 / 30.4 tok/s with the logits read back against 24.4 / 27.6 / 25.2 sampled on the device; top-k 40, top-p 0.95: 27.5 / 26.1 / 27.1 against 21.8 / 20.7 / 16.2. Lavapipe’s “device” is the CPU that would sample, and its barriers are costly, so this is no measure of a GPU; one draw alone at 262,144 tokens: 1.2 ms greedy, 4.0 ms at top-k 40, against 1.1 to 2.6 ms to copy the row and sample on the CPU.
  • Verdict: pending (a real GPU: the P1000, the RX 9060 XT of issue #461)
  • Could work when: every layer and the output are on the GPU (--gpu-layers all); most for small models with large vocabularies, where reading the logits back and sorting them is a large share of a step.

(b) Position-seeded counter-based RNG (Philox, Gumbel-max)

Section titled “(b) Position-seeded counter-based RNG (Philox, Gumbel-max)”
  • Source: uzu.
  • Date: 2026-10-09 · Inga version: 0.22.1-dev
  • Idea: draw each token’s random number from a counter-based generator seeded by the position (Philox) and sample by Gumbel-max, so speculative decoding gives exactly the text plain sampling gives with the same seed.
  • Built: inga_model::philox4x32 (Philox4x32-10, checked against Random123’s known answers), counter [token, stream, position low, position high], key the seed; the sampler keeps no random state: Sampler::sample_at draws the candidate with the largest (logit - max) / temperature plus its token’s Gumbel noise among those top-k, top-p, min-p, typical-p, top-n-sigma and XTC keep (XTC’s own draw is stream 1); mirostat keeps its target between tokens. The scan stops at the first candidate that cannot win (the noise is below 17), so a top-k of 0 does not draw for the whole vocabulary. Candidates are ordered by logit, then by lower id, so ties pick the same token on every device.
  • Measured on: synthetic models (crates/inga-engine/tests/session.rs): speculative decoding with prompt lookup and a draft model at temperature 0.8 gives the plain text (it did with the old generator too, as long as every call drew once per token), a second turn gives a fresh session’s text (it did not before); the draws over 40 000 positions follow the filtered distribution (crates/inga-model/tests/sampler.rs).
  • How: the cost of a draw (one Philox per candidate scanned, against one SplitMix64 per token before) on a real model, inga run / inga serve at temperature 0.8 with top-k 40 and with top-k 0, before and after; on the GPU runner with compare: base (.github/workflows/gpu.yml), a model in INGA_MODELS.
  • Result: not measured yet (inga bench does not sample).
  • Verdict: pending (adopted for its exactness; the speed check is pending)
  • Could work when: speculative decoding with temperature above 0 (draft models, prompt lookup, EAGLE-3 / MTP), and for reproducible GPU sampling ((a)).

(c) Vulkan barriers only where ranges overlap

Section titled “(c) Vulkan barriers only where ranges overlap”
  • Source: uzu (automatic hazard tracking).
  • Idea: track the buffer ranges each dispatch reads and writes, and put a barrier only where a read meets an earlier write, instead of after every dispatch.
  • Built (0.31.0, spec 2026-10-14-vulkan-barriers-design.md): whole buffers by handle, the written bindings from the SPIR-V (crates/inga-vulkan/src/hazard.rs, pipeline.rs), on by default; --vk-barriers all / --ab debug.vk_barriers=all go back.
  • Result (lavapipe, indicative only): a synthetic 28-layer Qwen3-shaped Q8_0 model (barriers_speed): a decode step 758 commands, 760 barriers before, 675 after (-11 %); a prompt chunk of 128 tokens 762 barriers before, 676 after. The speed did not move beyond lavapipe’s noise (decode 2.9-4.4 tokens/s on both sides across two ABAB rounds, the prompt 13.8-14.8). On the 3-layer test models a decode step drops 9 % (Llama Q4_K), 12 % (Qwen3 Q8_0) and 23 % (Gemma 3 F16): the quantized products each quantize their input into one shared buffer, which orders them; the F16 ones read it as it is, so Q, K and V, gate and up, overlap.
  • Verdict: pending the P1000 and RX 9060 XT measures (inga bench -p 512 -n 128 -r 3 --ab debug.vk_barriers=all); on lavapipe the bits are the same and the synchronization validation reports no hazard. Part 2 (spec §8) quantizes a norm’s output once for the products that read it (Q, K, V; gate, up; a shared expert’s; one token’s routed experts’ gate and up): the 28-layer model’s decode step drops to 112 quantizations (196 before), 535 barriers (675) and 674 commands (758), the same bits (norm_outputs_are_quantized_once, shared_input_speed); lavapipe’s speed stays within its noise. Real GPUs are still to be measured.
  • Could work when: a decode step records many small dispatches in one command buffer (Vulkan V3), on GPUs where barriers stall the queue.

A request alone under --parallel decodes as without a batcher

Section titled “A request alone under --parallel decodes as without a batcher”
  • Source: the maintainer’s Colab A100 (Inga 3bbb5222): a single streamed /v1/chat/completions request to inga serve --parallel 64 ran at 94 tokens/s on Qwen3-8B Q4_K_M and Qwen3-30B-A3B Q4_K_M, against inga bench tg128’s 136.6 and 147.9 (3.3–3.9 ms more per token); llama-server loses 141 → 125 there.
  • Idea: with a batcher, a session decoding alone still went through Model::forward_batch, which skips the GPU’s whole-model pass (layers, final norm and output layer in one graph): the hidden state read back, the norm on the CPU, a separate output layer, the ~600 KB of logits downloaded and sampled on the CPU, then copied three times and handed over through a condition variable. Now, while it is the only session in the decode loop with nothing queued (Batcher::alone), it runs its step itself with Model::forward_sampled as inga run and inga bench do (the token drawn on the GPU when the sampler allows it), holding the batcher’s turn; a session joining waits that step and batches from the next. The batched steps’ logits are copied once, to their session, instead of three times.
  • Measured: on the A100 (Colab, issue #966 part B, #902 05fed6d against main 3efd3c1; inga serve FILE --parallel 64 --ctx 768 --device cuda:0 --gpu-layers all, 8 streamed requests one after the other, 256 tokens, temperature 0): Qwen3-8B Q4_K_M 120.6 → 132.9 tokens/s (decode 126.2 → 139.7, TTFT p50 106.3 → 105.1 ms), Qwen3-30B-A3B Q4_K_M 120.1 → 132.5 (decode 126.1 → 139.9), the lone path taken on every decode step (2111 and 2126) with every token drawn on the GPU. Same output: the CPU tests give the same tokens with and without --parallel, greedy and seeded.
  • Verdict: kept (merged for 0.39.0, PR #902): about 10 % more for a lone request.
  • Could work when: one request at a time on a server started with --parallel (the default for a team), on every GPU backend whose output layer samples.

Split-KV decode attention on the CPU (debug.attn_split)

Section titled “Split-KV decode attention on the CPU (debug.attn_split)”
  • Source: llama.cpp’s CPU flash attention, which splits a long cache over the threads by chunks of 512 positions, and vLLM’s flash-decoding; the decode attention at 6.6 ms of a 512-token chat’s token on Qwen3-0.6B (exp/0.44, bench --profile), split over the threads by (token, KV head) only.
  • Idea: chunks of 256 positions on a fixed grid, each chunk’s online softmax a partial (maximum, sum, output not divided) merged in order by the log-sum-exp rescale; a decode call runs one task per (token, KV head, chunk) and merges on the calling thread (a second pool job to merge doubled the time on the loaded machine), and a prompt’s tiles make the same chunks and merges, so the bits do not depend on the threads or on batching.
  • Measured: the attention alone (crates/inga-cpu/tests/attn_bench.rs, f16 caches of 2048 and 4096 positions, a 4-vCPU Xeon with AVX-512 shared with other builds, medians of 11 alternated samples over 5 runs): 0.96–1.08× on one and two threads for Qwen3-0.6B’s, Qwen3-4B’s and Qwen2.5-0.5B’s heads; four threads not measurable (about 4 ms per call either way). Against the switch off, a relative difference of at most 4.1e-8.
  • Verdict: kept behind debug.attn_split, off until measured on a whole token.
  • Could work when: more threads than KV heads (Qwen2.5-0.5B’s 2 on 4 or more threads), or threads that a busy machine leaves unevenly loaded.
