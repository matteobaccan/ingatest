A mixture's experts on the GPU without a round trip to the CPU per layer (llama.cpp's MULMATID)

The 0.38.0 roadmap’s item “A mixture’s experts on the GPU without a round trip to the CPU per layer” (docs/todo/0.38.0.md), the step left by 2026-10-22-cuda-ggml-small-kernels-design.md (“As built”) and part 3 of 2026-10-22-cuda-ggml-mmvq-design.md (“The parts”). Ported as MMQ (2026-10-19-cuda-ggml-mmq-design.md), MMVQ and flash attention (2026-10-22-cuda-ggml-fattn-design.md) were: ggml’s kernels unchanged (MIT, THIRD-PARTY-NOTICES.md), compiled to PTX by tools/build-ggml-ptx.sh from llama.cpp b11489, launched as ggml’s host code launches them, Inga’s own path kept as the fallback and the default until a GPU measures it.

Why

Section titled “Why”

Measured on the maintainer’s Colab A100-SXM4-40GB on 2026-10-23 (Inga main de9d9485, llama.cpp b11489, every layer on the GPU, 3 repetitions): Qwen3-30B-A3B Q4_K_M tg128 runs at 40.4 tokens/s against llama.cpp’s 175.8 (0.23). The router on the GPU (debug.cuda_moe_router) did not help (39.7): with it the layer still goes back to the CPU once per MoE layer, for the experts’ ids. Batched sessions gain little for the same reason (--users 8: 1.10× with the batched attention, against 1.65–1.78× on dense models). Each of Qwen3-30B-A3B’s 48 MoE layers today: the attention on the GPU, the hidden state read back, the norm on the CPU, the router’s logits and top-k (on the GPU with debug.cuda_moe_router, read back), the token sent again for its experts, their sum read back, the residual added on the CPU, the hidden state sent for the next layer: two or three synchronizations a layer, and no decode step recorded as one graph.

What llama.cpp does (read on 2026-10-23, llama.cpp b11489, commit 06cad0b9)

Section titled “What llama.cpp does (read on 2026-10-23, llama.cpp b11489, commit 06cad0b9)”

llm_graph_context::build_moe_ffn (src/llama-graph.cpp) builds a MoE layer as graph nodes that never leave the device: the router’s MUL_MAT, the softmax (or sigmoid), ggml_argsort_top_k into an I32 tensor of ids (the argsort’s [n_expert, n_tokens], the first n_expert_used of each row used), the weights by GET_ROWS, then MUL_MAT_ID for the gate, the up and the down projections on the stacked experts (ffn_gate_exps, [n_embd, n_ff, n_expert]), the GLU, the weights multiplied (ggml_mul(experts, weights)) and the experts’ outputs added one after the other (ggml_add). The CUDA backend (ggml/src/ggml-cuda/) runs them as:

  • ggml-cuda.cu‘s fusion: softmax/argsort/top-k/weights as one topk_moe_cuda (topk-moe.cu, already ported: debug.cuda_moe_router), and the weights’ MUL with the chain of ADDs as one moe_weighted_reduction_f32 (moe-weighted-reduction.cu, ggml_cuda_match_moe_weighted_reduction, 2 to 15 experts a token).
  • ggml_cuda_mul_mat_id for each MUL_MAT_ID: for at most get_mmvq_mmid_max_batch(type, cc) tokens (8 on Volta and from Ada on; the Turing-and-newer table from Turing, 5 to 8; Pascal’s below, 4 to 6) its matrix-vector kernels with the ids (ggml_cuda_mul_mat_vec_q(ctx, src0, src1, ids, dst)): one token through mul_mat_vec_q<type, 1, ...> with a block row per slot and ids[slot] naming the expert, several through the dedicated mul_mat_vec_q_moe<type, 2> (a warp a token); more tokens through MMQ by ids (ggml_cuda_mul_mat_q with ids, where ggml_cuda_should_use_mmq says so: Pascal, Turing and newer; Volta’s large batches go to cuBLAS): mm_ids_helper (mmid.cu) sorts the tokens’ slots by expert on the device (each expert’s bounds), quantize_mmq_q8_1 writes the activations in that order (quantize_scatter_mmq_q8_1_cuda: each token’s row quantized once for all its slots when the slots share it, the gate and up products), and mul_mat_q multiplies each expert’s rows by its matrix (expert_bounds, ids_dst). No path reads the ids on the host except the fallback ggml asserts away when it records graphs (ggml_cuda_mul_mat_id_needs_sync).
  • With fusion on, MMVQ also fuses the gate and up products with the GLU, and a shared expert into the same launch (fusion.shared_up, ggml_cuda_match_shared_expert).

Decisions

Each MMVQ module (mmvq-TYPE.smARCH.ptx.z) also keeps its type’s mul_mat_vec_q_moe<type, 2, has_fusion = false>; its launch bounds are the PTX’s architecture’s table (get_mmvq_mmid_max_batch_for_device), read from its .maxntid when the module loads. The MMQ modules already held mul_mat_q (which takes the ids when expert_bounds is not null) and both quantize_mmq_q8_1 variants: unchanged.
  • Launched as ggml launches them: ggml_cuda_mul_mat_vec_q’s strides for MUL_MAT_ID (the token’s columns s12/s2, each slot’s s11/s1, each expert’s s02, ids_stride = n_expert), small_k for one token as pick already chooses it, mul_mat_vec_q_moe_launch’s grid (rows / 2, k) and block (32, tokens); ggml_cuda_mul_mat_q’s mmq_args for ids (ncols_dst = ncols_y = tokens × k, nchannels_x = nchannels_y = n_expert, ncols_max = ncols_opt = tokens), the tile of the fewest tiles for the tokens (Inga’s 16/32/64/128 widths, as for MMQ), stream-k and its fixup where ggml’s table says; launch_moe_weighted_reduction’s (tokens, d / 256) grid of 256 threads.
  • ggml’s choice between them: get_mmvq_mmid_max_batch written as ggml’s host code has it (NVIDIA’s part, mmid_max), within the PTX’s launch bounds (sm_61 PTX runs on Volta: Pascal’s table there). Past it, MMQ by ids, also on Volta where ggml would take cuBLAS (Inga has no cuBLAS).
  • Not taken in this step: MMVQ’s fusion (has_fusion: the gate and up products with the GLU in one launch, the shared expert in the same launch), f16/bf16 experts (ggml’s MMF), the experts’ scales (ffn_down_exps.scale, Gemma 4) in the weighted sum. They come after the measure.

How the ids stay on the device

Section titled “How the ids stay on the device”

A layer that takes the path (ids_blocker, below) uploads its experts stacked as llama.cpp stores them (GpuOps::upload_experts: one buffer per projection, expert after expert, padded once at the end as ggml pads the tensor), its router, its selection bias and its norm; its experts are not uploaded one by one (no memory twice). Each session’s scratch, made when the session opens (before any decode step is recorded), holds the layer’s logits, ids and weights ([rows, n_expert], [rows, n_expert], [rows, k]), the slots’ gate, up and down outputs ([rows × k, n_ff], [rows × k, n_embd]) and the room of the products’ quantized inputs (GpuOps::ids_scratch). The top-k writes the ids there (GpuOps::topk_ids: int32s in an f32 buffer, inga_gpu::id_bits), the products read them there (GpuOps::matmul_ids), and nothing is read back: the layer leaves nothing to the CPU, so RemoteAttention::ffn says RemoteFfn::Dense, the CPU’s layer loop adds nothing, consecutive layers run in one call (RemoteAttention::span), and a decode step through all of them is recorded as one CUDA graph and replayed.

The weighted sum

Section titled “The weighted sum”

moe_weighted_reduction_f32: each token’s k outputs times their weights, added in slot order (the order the top-k picked them), as the CPU’s finish_moe adds its slots (y × w, then the sum from the first). ggml fuses it for 2 to 15 experts a token (its graph matcher’s limit); the kernel takes any number, so Inga launches it for every k. The sum goes to s.o, the shared expert’s output is added to it, and the result to the residual stream (s.h): the CPU’s order (ff = routed, ff += shared, h += ff).

Shared experts (Qwen2-MoE, Qwen3.5-MoE, Qwen3-Next)

Section titled “Shared experts (Qwen2-MoE, Qwen3.5-MoE, Qwen3-Next)”

The shared expert already runs beside the attention (Model::plain_ffn, RemoteFfn::Shared, its output read back for the CPU to add). In a layer that runs whole, it runs on the same normed input before the router (its gate and up products through the input quantized once, its sigmoid gate ffn_gate_inp_shexp), its output is kept on the device and added to the routed experts’ sum: nothing goes back. ggml’s fusion of the shared expert into the routed experts’ MMVQ launch is not taken (above).

Which layers take the path

Section titled “Which layers take the path”

Model::plain_moe(l) hands a layer’s feed-forward block over whole: its norm, a router Model::plain_router hands over (softmax, the softmax of the k chosen, sigmoid, sqrt-softplus, DeepSeek V3’s selection bias; not a router with a bias, groups of experts, Gemma 4’s, hashed routing), SwiGLU or clamped SwiGLU experts without biases or scales, and the shared expert if any (not a clamped one). It refuses what the CPU does between the experts and the residual stream: norms after the block, a residual scale (Granite). The GPU side (ids_blocker in inga-gpu/src/layers.rs) adds: debug.cuda_moe_ids on, a device that keeps the ids (GpuOps::runs_expert_ids: CUDA with llama.cpp’s MMVQ and MMQ on and the small kernels’ module loaded), a top-k it runs (the numbers of experts ggml instantiates: 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, 64, 128, 256, 288, 512, 576), experts of one type and shape, the same experts, experts per token and width as the stage’s other such layers, and types both the matrix-vector kernels and MMQ take by ids (Q4_0, Q8_0, Q4_K, Q5_K, Q6_K; the others with debug.cuda_ggml_new_types; MMQ’s rows a multiple of 128).

  • Covered: Qwen3-MoE (Qwen3-30B-A3B: 128 experts, 8 a token), OLMoE (64, 8), Mixtral (Llama’s layers with 8 experts, 2 a token), Qwen2-MoE with 64 experts (Qwen2-57B-A14B; Qwen1.5-MoE-A2.7B’s 60 experts are not a number ggml’s top-k instantiates: old path), Qwen3.5-MoE (its DeltaNet layers on the device too, shared experts). Qwen3-Next (512 experts, 10 a token, shared experts) passes the same checks; the tests do not run it.
  • Not in this step: Gemma 4 26B-A4B. Its router reads its own norm of the residual, its experts have an output scale and its block has norms after the experts and the dense network beside them: plain_router and plain_moe refuse it, and its layer stays on the old path (remote_span keeps its layers apart already). It is the next case once this one is measured.
  • The old path stays for what the router setting already leaves on the CPU (gpt-oss’s router and experts with biases, DeepSeek V3/R1’s groups, DeepSeek V4’s hashed and clamped layers, Nemotron-H’s squared-ReLU experts, LoRA adapters), for experts in f16, bf16 or a type MMQ does not take, and for any layer when the setting is off. A layer on the old path with the setting on still has its router on the GPU (the setting implies debug.cuda_moe_router).

Every placement says what it did (the transparency rule): with the setting on, the GPU log has “N of M MoE layers whole on the GPU, the experts on their ids (llama.cpp’s MUL_MAT_ID, debug.cuda_moe_ids)” and, for the first layer that is not, why.

Batched sessions (--users)

Section titled “Batched sessions (--users)”

A batched decode step (RemoteAttention::forward_batch, group_step) runs the layer’s rest through the same after_mix for all its rows: the router and top-k for every session’s token, the products by ids on rows tokens (the matrix-vector kernels up to ggml’s tokens, MMQ by ids past them), the weighted sum per token. The experts are read once a step for every session, as llama.cpp’s server reads them, where the old path ran each expert’s rows (and went back to the CPU) per layer. Mixed steps (H3) give the prompts’ chunks to the same path (MMQ by ids).

Prompts

Section titled “Prompts”

A prompt’s chunk takes MMQ by ids for its gate, up and down products; the grouped path of GPU prefill P4 (each expert’s tokens gathered on the host’s routes) is not used by a layer that runs whole. Its room grows with the chunk: scratch_bytes counts the layer’s buffers for every chunk size when the setting is on, so prefill_chunk picks a smaller chunk where they do not fit.

What a layer that runs whole does not do

Section titled “What a layer that runs whole does not do”

The experts’ use profile (Model::keep_expert_profile, which pins streamed experts) and the prerouter’s trace record the routes the CPU computes: a layer whose routes stay on the GPU adds nothing to them. Its experts are in GPU memory, so pinning does not concern them; collecting a prerouter trace runs with the setting off. RemoteAttention::experts still works on such a layer (the CPU’s routes written as its ids), for callers that route on the CPU.

The setting: debug.cuda_moe_ids

Section titled “The setting: debug.cuda_moe_ids”

A key of inga.toml and --set (src/config/keys.rs, docs/config.md), read when the GPU opens (CUDA’s small kernels’ module) and when the experts are placed. Decided by the maintainer on 2026-10-23, after the A100’s measure: a measured switch (inga_cuda::measured_setting), as debug.cuda_mmvq, debug.cuda_ggml_small and debug.cuda_batch_attention. Unset, it is on for CUDA GPUs of compute capability 8.0 and newer and off below them and on HIP (and every other device: GpuOps::runs_expert_ids decides, HostOps only when it is set to true); a value the user sets wins either way. The router on the GPU it implies follows the same rule (a layer that cannot run whole still has its router there where the device keeps the ids); debug.cuda_moe_router set to true keeps its own meaning. A CUDA GPU that takes the default says so to the plans made without the device (inga_gpu::note_expert_ids), which count the layers’ room (expert_bytes, scratch_bytes). Gpu::set_moe_ids turns it on or off for tests; GpuAttentionStages::whole_moe_layers lists the layers that took it.

The tests

Section titled “The tests”
  • Without a GPU (cargo test -p inga-gpu --test moe_ids, -p inga-cuda --lib): HostOps keeps the ids (topk_ids writes the CPU’s own top-k as ids, matmul_ids multiplies each slot’s row by its expert through the reference backend, as the CPU’s experts do, moe_weighted_sum adds the slots as finish_moe): synthetic Qwen3-MoE, OLMoE, Qwen2-MoE, Qwen3.5-MoE and Mixtral models give the CPU’s logits within 1e-5, every MoE layer takes the path (whole_moe_layers, RemoteFfn::Dense, the top-k counted once per layer and pass), gpt-oss’s does not; three sessions in batched steps give each session’s and the CPU’s; RemoteAttention::experts on a layer whole gives the old path’s sum; with the setting off every layer keeps the old path, and Gemma 4’s and gpt-oss’s layers are not handed over whole. debug.cuda_moe_ids is a key of inga.toml read off by default (src/config/tests.rs). The PTX modules hold the new kernels with ggml’s parameters and launch bounds (small.rs, mmvq.rs, ggml.rs’ tests), mmid_max is ggml’s table.
  • On a GPU (INGA_CUDA=1 cargo test -p inga-cuda --release --test moe_ids -- --ignored --nocapture):
    • llama_cpp_products_by_ids_match_ggml_s_arithmetic: Q8_0, Q4_K, Q5_K, Q6_K experts of 2048 → 768, 768 → 2048 (16 experts, 8 a token) and 1024 → 1024 (8, 2), 1 to 130 tokens, a row a token and a row a slot, against ggml’s arithmetic (block_q8_1 activations, the integer dot products scaled in f32) in f64: 1e-4 of the largest output through the matrix-vector kernels, 1e-2 through MMQ (its f16 scales);
    • the_top_k_kept_on_the_gpu_and_the_weighted_sum: the ids and weights kept equal the ones the top-k reads back; the weighted sum within 1e-6 of the f64 sum;
    • whole_moe_layers_on_the_gpu_give_the_cpus_logits: the five architectures in Q8_0 (and Q4_K for three), nine sessions (prompts of 3 to 40 tokens: the several-token kernel and MMQ by ids, then three decode steps, recorded and replayed alone, and batched as a group of 8 and one), every MoE layer whole, and three criteria, each with its reason: (a) against the CPU’s logits (4e-2 of the largest, 8e-2 for Q4_K) for the sessions whose router stays at least 1 % of its logits’ spread from a tie on the CPU: the path’s arithmetic against the reference, where the GPU’s rounding cannot pick another expert; (b) every session alone against itself batched, within 2e-2: a batched step’s own errors (another session’s row, ids or weights, a slot’s order) show there whatever the routers’ margins; (c) every session of at most 8 tokens alone against the GPU’s old path alone (the router there too), within 2e-2 (as (b), below the CPU’s 4e-2 / 8e-2; a wrong expert’s rows, ids or slot order part them by about 1e-1): the same kernel family, equal to the bit where both run the same kernels on the same rows, a recorded step’s or the shared expert’s and hybrid layers’ order rounding a few sessions apart. The old path’s batched steps are no reference (they cross near ties of their own), nor are the longer prompts against it (MMQ by ids against the grouped products: a tie between two GPU paths). Every session is listed with its margin.
  • The speed, on the A100 (Colab, the cell colab-038-moe.py), by the rule of docs/performance.md: Qwen3-30B-A3B Q4_K_M tg128 and pp512 with --set debug.cuda_moe_ids=false|true beside llama-bench b11489, and --users 1,4,8 -p 128 -n 128 -r 1 off and on. Then the P1000 (sm_61, Pascal’s table), before the default changes below 8.0.
