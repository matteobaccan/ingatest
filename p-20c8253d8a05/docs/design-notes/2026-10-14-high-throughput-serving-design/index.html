Many users at once: batched decode on the GPU, paged KV, batched prompts

Status: proposed for 0.35.0 (decided 2026-10-14). First proposal (from docs/what-inga-replaces.md: “not replaced yet: high-throughput multi-GPU servers (vLLM, SGLang, TGI) for many concurrent users”, and “keep Text Embeddings Inference / Infinity if you serve them at high throughput on many GPUs”). The several-GPU half is the tensor and expert parallelism spec; this spec is the many-users half. Code: crates/inga-engine/src/batch.rs (Batcher, PassGate), crates/inga-server/src/pool.rs, crates/inga-model/src/model.rs (KvCache, Model::forward_batch), crates/inga-gpu/src/layers.rs (GpuStages, GpuLayers), crates/inga-gpu/src/ops.rs (GpuOps), the CUDA, Vulkan, HIP and Metal kernels’ attention.

What exists today (read from the code)

Section titled “What exists today (read from the code)”
  • Continuous batching of decode (batch.rs): with --parallel N, the sessions’ decode steps run as one Model::forward_batch, weights read once per step.
  • Only on the CPU. pool.rs creates the batcher only when engine.stages.is_none(); forward_batch refuses caches with stages (KvCache::is_distributed). A model with layers on a GPU (a GpuStages stage) runs its N sessions one at a time on the device: N users get about 1/N of the speed each.
  • Prompts are not batched: each session processes its own; --prefill-yield interleaves them with decode (PassGate).
  • KV caches are contiguous per session: KvCache { len, cap }, each session reserving ctx / parallel-style room up front; no sharing of a common prefix between sessions except through the prefix cache’s copies.
  • Admission control and a response cache exist (admission.rs, response_cache.rs, 0.30.0 work).

Decisions (2026-10-14)

Section titled “Decisions (2026-10-14)”
  1. Scope: H1–H3 (batched decode on the GPU, paged KV, batched prompts) for a team (10–50 users on one or two GPUs); a datacenter server stays out.
  2. --parallel: auto by default for inga serve (as many sessions as the KV pages allow, at most 32).
  3. Embeddings throughput (H4): after the embeddings part 2 spec.

1. What the user sees

Section titled “1. What the user sees”
  • inga serve MODEL --parallel 16 on a GPU: 16 users decode together; total tokens per second grow with users until the GPU is compute-bound (the goal: 8 users at 5x one user’s total throughput on a 7B Q4 model on an RTX-class GPU).
  • /metrics and GET /api/inga/system: batch size per step, KV pages used / free, prompts batched.
  • inga bench MODEL --users 1,4,16 --prompt 512 --gen 128: a load test against the loaded model with total and per-user tokens per second (as vLLM’s benchmark_serving), a row in docs/performance.md.

2. Design

Section titled “2. Design”

H1 — batched decode on GPU stages

Section titled “H1 — batched decode on GPU stages”
  • RemoteLayers gets a batched entry, forward_batch(tokens, caches), implemented by GpuStages: the device’s matrix products take N rows (the prompt kernels already take many rows: the tiled Q8_1 products of the gpu-prefill spec), and attention runs per sequence on its own cache (a batched decode-attention kernel with a per-row cache pointer and length).
  • forward_batch in model.rs accepts caches whose stages are local GPU stages (not remote machines), and pool.rs creates the batcher whenever every stage is local.
  • Hybrid placements (some layers on the CPU, some on the GPU) batch both halves.

H2 — paged KV

Section titled “H2 — paged KV”
  • KvLayer’s storage becomes pages of 16 positions (KvPages) taken from one pool per device and model; a session’s cache is a list of page ids. vLLM’s PagedAttention idea, in Inga’s own layout (F16, Q8_0, the quantized KV types).
  • Prefix sharing: a prefix already in another session’s pages is shared by reference (pages counted, copied on write only at the last partial page). This replaces the prefix cache’s copies for live sessions; the disk tier (kv_disk.rs) stays.
  • Admission (admission.rs) counts free pages instead of sessions: a request waits when its prompt’s pages are not free; the longest-idle session’s pages are swapped to RAM (the KV offload spec’s K1–K2) before a request is refused.
  • Recurrent and hybrid models keep their state per session (not paged), only their attention layers’ KV is paged.

H3 — batched prompts (chunked prefill mixed with decode)

Section titled “H3 — batched prompts (chunked prefill mixed with decode)”
  • A step takes up to B tokens: every decoding session’s next token plus chunks of waiting prompts, in one pass (SGLang / vLLM’s chunked prefill). PassGate’s turns become the step’s composition; --prefill-yield becomes the chunk size.
  • Attention handles rows of different sequences in one pass (the batched attention of H1 with per-row lengths).

H4 — embeddings and rerank batched across requests

Section titled “H4 — embeddings and rerank batched across requests”
  • /v1/embeddings and /v1/rerank requests arriving within a few milliseconds are joined into one pass of Model::encode (one input at a time today, bert.rs for the encoders), padded to the longest input, on the GPU when the model is there.

3. Tests

Section titled “3. Tests”
  • forward_batch with GPU stages against N separate forward calls, on HostOps (no GPU) and on CUDA / Vulkan with #[ignore] device tests: logits within the batched-kernel tolerance.
  • Paged KV: a session’s output with pages equals the contiguous cache’s, for every KV type; shared prefixes with copy-on-write; swap-out and back.
  • tests/server.rs: 16 concurrent streams on the synthetic model, each answer equal to the answer given alone (greedy).
  • The load test on the P1000 and a tester’s GPU, rows in docs/performance.md.

4. Phases and effort

Section titled “4. Phases and effort”
  • H1 (L): batched decode on GPU stages, CUDA and Vulkan first, Metal and HIP next.
  • H2 (L): paged KV, prefix sharing, admission by pages.
  • H3 (M): mixed steps of prompt chunks and decode.
  • H4 (S): embeddings and rerank batched across requests.
  • H5 (S): inga bench --users, the metrics.

5. Risks, licences, size

Section titled “5. Risks, licences, size”
  • Paged KV touches every attention kernel on every backend: the largest risk; H1 and H3 are useful without it (contiguous caches with a fixed --parallel).
  • Batched kernels round differently from single-row ones: answers stay within the existing tolerances, not bit-equal; --parallel 1 keeps today’s path.
  • No new crate; kernels in the existing PTX / SPIR-V / Metal sources.

As built

Section titled “As built”

H5 — inga bench --users (2026-10-16)

Section titled “H5 — inga bench --users (2026-10-16)”
  • src/commands/bench/users.rs. Each count’s sessions are real Sessions, started together behind a barrier, and run as the server’s pool runs them. They share a Batcher when Engine::batches_decode() allows it, the one place that decides (pool.rs asks it too); otherwise Engine::batch_blocker() gives the exact reason, which the header line and the JSON’s batch_blocker show, and inga serve --parallel N writes in its log (since 0.33.1, issue #401). Sampling is greedy, with the end-of-text tokens banned through logit_bias so that every user generates -n tokens. TTFT and ITL are timed on the streamed pieces, as vLLM’s benchmark_serving does. --json gives one object per count.

H1 — batched decode on GPU stages, first part (2026-10-16)

Section titled “H1 — batched decode on GPU stages, first part (2026-10-16)”
  • RemoteLayers::forward_batch(others, hidden), with batches() and as_any_mut(), and StageSource::batches_decode(). GpuLayers implements them; the first stage downcasts the other caches’ stages of the same Shared and runs them together (decode_batch in crates/inga-gpu/src/layers.rs).
  • Attention runs per session, without a new kernel. After the batched Q/K/V products (attn_project), each session’s rows are copied into its own one-row scratch (GpuOps::copy_part, a new operation for CUDA / HIP, Vulkan and HostOps). There attn_core runs RoPE, kv_store and attention with that session’s position, RoPE table and cache, as a step alone does. The output is copied back to the batch’s rows for attn_output and the feed-forward network (after_mix).
  • At most max_rows (8) sessions per pass; more run in groups of eight, and a group of one takes the session’s own (graph-recorded) step. GpuHead::logits takes up to eight rows in one product.
    • Groups beyond eight (2026-10-24, CUDA). A device with GpuOps::big_groups (CUDA: debug.cuda_big_groups, unset on from compute capability 8.0, off below and on HIP; Vulkan, Metal and WebGPU unchanged) runs, on layers without a mixture’s experts on the device (groups_past_rows: a mixture keeps groups of eight, measured slower in groups of 16 and 24), groups of whole multiples of eight from 16 to MAX_GROUP (64) and the smallest session’s prompt chunk (inga_gpu::decode_groups: 24 sessions one group, 28 a group of 24 and one of 4, 100 groups of 64, 32 and 4). A group’s scratch is its first session’s: the powers of two are reserved with the prompt chunks’, other sizes made on first use and kept; where the device has no room, that group goes in the powers of two (inga_gpu::pow2_groups). Such a group’s products take GpuOps::matmul (MMQ, tiles) as a prompt chunk’s (attn_project, attn_output and the recurrent layers’ products with prompt = rows > max_rows; the feed-forward network and the mixtures’ layers whole already did); CUDA’s rope_rows, kv_store_rows and attention_rows take any number of rows, eight a launch (kernels/batch.cu unchanged); GpuHead::logits takes the powers of two (pow2_groups), one product each (its rooms of 16 to 64 rows made on first use, eight rows at a time when the device has no room for them). GpuStages::set_big_groups(Some(false)) (and GpuAttentionStages‘) keeps the old groups, for comparison. Fewer than 16 sessions run as before. H1 — the rest: a batched decode-attention kernel, mixtures and hybrid models (2026-10-22)

Section titled “H1 — the rest: a batched decode-attention kernel, mixtures and hybrid models (2026-10-22)”
  • The batched decode-attention kernel. GpuOps::rope_rows, kv_store_rows and attention_rows (with batches_attention()) take a group’s rows, row j with its own position and KV cache: four launches per layer for the group (RoPE of the queries, of the keys, the cache store, the attention) instead of about eight per session (three copies in, RoPE twice, the store, the attention, one copy out). rows_core in crates/inga-gpu/src/layers.rs runs them when the device has them, set_rows_attention(true) (the default; GpuStages::set_rows_attention(false) keeps the per-session copies, for comparison) and every session’s cache holds as many positions (one rotary table for the group: Phi-3’s depends on it); else the copies, as before. The KV cache layout is unchanged (H2 pages it).
    • Each row’s arithmetic is the single-sequence kernel’s for that row alone, in the same order: the same bits (inga_gpu::testing::batched_attention_rows, two steps, 1, 3 and 8 rows at positions up to past a tile of 256, windows, soft-capping, sinks, both rotary layouts, heads of 64 to 512).
    • Vulkan: shaders/rope_rows.comp, kv_store_rows.comp, attention_rows.comp. Each session’s cache is a buffer of its own at a binding of its own (8 keys, 8 values: 19 bindings for the attention), picked by a switch on the row (uniform in a workgroup), so no device feature for arrays of buffers is needed. The two shaders that bind the caches (BATCH_SHADERS) are built only where maxPerStageDescriptorStorageBuffers and maxDescriptorSetStorageBuffers allow 19 (desktop drivers allow thousands, the specification guarantees 4); elsewhere the copies. MAX_BINDINGS 5 → 19, the written-bindings masks 8 → 32 bits, the push constants 64 → 128 bytes (rope_rows’s 18 words; Vulkan guarantees 128). On by default; bit for bit on lavapipe. debug.vk_batch_attention = false turns them off (the copies, as before: a way out on a driver where they misbehave, since only lavapipe has run them; tests/batch_attention_off.rs).

Rows of up to flash_min_len positions (32; 256 for blocks of 8 heads; debug.cuda_flash_decode_min) stay on attention_f16 / attention_rows_f16, launched beside it: each block reads its row’s length on the device and returns when the row is the other kernel’s (max_len, min_len). Its room (8 MB and the counters) is made with the GPU. Heads of 512 and prompts keep attention_f16. Tests: crates/inga-cuda/tests/attention.rs’s split_k_decode_* and rows_either_side_of_the_split_k_threshold_*; measured on the A100 (docs/experiments.md), the fourth version not yet.
  • Mixtures and hybrid models (the attention-only placements, GpuAttentionStages) batch too. RemoteAttention::batches, as_any_mut and forward_batch(l, n, pos, others, h, shared): the device’s layers l..l + n (a span, as forward_span) for every session’s row, with the positions given (a remote attention counts its length per layer call). H4 — embeddings and rerank batched across requests (2026-10-22)

Section titled “H4 — embeddings and rerank batched across requests (2026-10-22)”
  • One pass for several inputs: Encoder::forward_many (crates/inga-model/src/bert.rs, Model::encode_many) packs the inputs’ rows one after the other, without padding (the spec said padded to the longest; packing wastes no rows and needs no mask). The embeddings, projections, norms and feed-forward network run once over all the rows; each input’s positions start at 0 and its attention (and nomic-bert’s RoPE) runs on its own rows only. Encoder::forward is forward_many of one input, the same code as before for it.
  • crates/inga-engine/src/encode.rs: EncoderInput (tokens, types), and Engine::encode_many (passes of at most ENCODE_BATCH_TOKENS, 4,096 tokens, the bound on the activations’ memory). Engine::embed_texts(texts, pooling, run) and Engine::rerank_with(query, docs, instruction, run) prepare every input (tokens, checks, a cross-encoder’s sentence types), hand them to run together and pool or score its rows; Engine::embed and Engine::rerank are them with encode_many as run, so a request with several texts, and a reranking of several documents, is one pass even outside the server. Decoders used as embedding models or rerankers keep their one-input-at-a-time path (run is not called).
  • crates/inga-server/src/encode_batch.rs: Batcher, in State (encoders), the run of /v1/embeddings (and /api/v0/embeddings, Azure’s), llama.cpp’s /embedding, Ollama’s /api/embed and /api/embeddings and the rerank routes (/v1/rerank, /rerank, /v1/reranking, /reranking). One queue per model (by the Engine’s address), no thread of its own: the request that finds its model’s queue idle leads a pass. Alone, it waits up to GATHER (2 ms) for others or until 4,096 tokens wait; with company already queued (requests that arrived during the last pass) it starts at once. It takes the queued requests in order up to 4,096 tokens (one larger request alone), runs Engine::encode_many on all their inputs, and hands each request its rows; the next pass is led by one of the requests still waiting. A failing pass is run again request by request, so an error stays its request’s; a panic gives each request of the pass an error instead of a hung queue.
  • Each request’s options stay its own: Ollama’s truncation (truncate, num_ctx) is applied to the texts before they are queued and its dimensions checked after, pooling and normalization are applied to each input’s own rows.
  • /metrics: inga_encoder_batches_total, inga_encoder_batch_requests_total, inga_encoder_batch_inputs_total (and encoder_batches, encoder_batch_requests, encoder_batch_inputs in the JSON of GET /api/inga/status).
  • Tests: encode_batch::tests (six concurrent requests in one pass, each vector within 1e-5 of the one given alone, and bit for bit in practice on the synthetic F32 encoder; Cls and mean pooling in the same pass; a bad input’s error kept to its request; cross-encoder scores of a request of one document and one of six in the same pass against alone); encode::tests::token_groups_keep_order_and_budget; tests/server/openai.rs, concurrent_embeddings_and_rerankings_share_passes_and_match_alone (eight clients embedding and one reranking at once against inga serve on the synthetic BERT, each answer within 1e-5 of the one alone, a list of eight texts likewise, and the /metrics counters).

H2 — paged KV, first part: the CPU and Vulkan (2026-10-22)

Section titled “H2 — paged KV, first part: the CPU and Vulkan (2026-10-22)”
  • Pages on the CPU. inga_core::KvPool (crates/inga-core/src/kv/pages.rs): pages of KV_PAGE (16) positions, one pool per model (Model::new_kv_pool, Model::set_kv_pool; the engine makes it when it assembles a model, debug.kv_paged, on by default). KvLayer::paged keeps a layer’s keys and values as a list of pages (Store::Paged), each page a contiguous f16 or block store of 16 rows: a position reads the bits a contiguous cache holds, for every KV type (f16, q8_0, and the rotated q4_0, q5_0, q4_k). Pages are taken as positions are written and given back by truncate and when the layer drops; a pool keeps 256 spare pages for reuse (no allocation in the steady state). Only the plain attention layers are paged (Model::paged_layers): recurrent states, indexer keys, compressed attention and layers split between RAM and disk (--kv-ram) stay contiguous, as the spec’s last bullet asks for the recurrent ones.
  • Sharing. A page is held by reference (PageRef, counted: KvPoolStats::shared). KvLayer::copy_prefix (KvCache::snapshot) shares the pages instead of copying them, and KvLayer::load_from (KvCache::restore) takes them by reference from a layer of the same pool; the first holder that writes to a shared page copies it (copy on write: only a partial last page in practice). This replaces the prefix cache’s copies for paged caches, as the spec asks; --kv-disk’s files are unchanged (the same bytes, KvLayer::write_to).
  • One prefix cache per server model. The sessions of pool.rs share one PrefixCache (Session::share_prefix_cache, the whole --prefix-cache budget; its entries carry the Session::kv_check they were made with, so a request’s LoRA weights never meet another’s keys). After a prompt, a session puts its whole pages into it by reference (Session::publish): another session whose prompt starts the same way (a system prompt, a document) restores them instead of processing them. At the end of a job, a session whose cache shares pages moves its conversation there (Session::release) and empties its cache, so an idle session holds no pages of its own; the conversation comes back by reference when it continues.
  • Admission and eviction. inga serve --kv-pages N (serve.kv_pages): the pool’s pages (default: --parallel times the context, plus the prefix cache’s budget in pages: nothing waits, as before). A job is promised the pages its prompt and max_tokens may need within the context (at most the pool); it starts when the promises of the jobs running leave room, else it waits in the queue (admission::next_waiter picks among the jobs that fit; one passed over MAX_PASSED times holds the others back until it fits; --max-queued-* still refuse). The promises only bound the memory: pages are taken as written. A full pool asks the prefix cache for pages back (KvPool::set_reclaim, set by Session::set_prefix_cache): its least recently used copy is dropped, written to --kv-disk first when there is one; the hook never waits for a lock (a step holding the cache’s own lock gets no page back). Before a job runs, idle sessions whose caches are not in the prefix cache (a GPU’s layers) are parked on the disk tier and emptied while the pool lacks the job’s pages (Pool::make_room). A page refused anyway is a 503 (Error::is_kv_pool_full). The spec’s “swapped to RAM” is, on the CPU, the prefix cache itself; vLLM’s preemption (recomputing a running request) is not done: the promises make it unneeded.
  • Vulkan. GpuOps::kv_pool, kv_cache_in, kv_pool_pages; StageSource::set_kv_pages, kv_pages. crates/inga-vulkan/src/layer.rs: KvPages (one per layer: the keys and values of N pages, the free list) and DeviceKv’s KvStore::Paged (its page ids and a table of them on the device, new entries written by vkCmdUpdateBuffer in order with the work recorded before). Shaders kv_store_paged.comp, attention_paged.comp, kv_store_rows_paged.comp, attention_rows_paged.comp: attention.comp’s arithmetic with position j’s row at table[j / 16] * 16 + j % 16, the same bits; one pool binding for the batch’s rows (and one table each) instead of eight caches. Built with the batched shaders (devices that bind 19 buffers); debug.vk_paged_kv (on) turns them off; a pool larger than maxStorageBufferRange, or refused by the driver, leaves contiguous caches (said in the log). The placement makes the pools when it opens the sessions (open_sessions: --parallel times the context, the memory it planned); --kv-pages makes them smaller, never larger (pool.rs). The unchanged SPIR-V stays byte-identical (four new files, four lines of spv/sources.sha256).
  • Metrics. /metrics: inga_kv_pages_total, inga_kv_pages_used, inga_kv_pages_shared; GET /api/inga/status: metrics.kv_pages (sums and each model’s total, used, shared, peak): the GPU’s pool where the model’s layers are (Engine::kv_pages), else the CPU’s.
  • Tests:
    • crates/inga-core/src/kv.rs: a paged layer reads the bits of a contiguous one for every type, through appends across pages, write_k, remove, truncate, saved and read back both ways, by ranges; pages given back and taken again; a full pool refuses and keeps the positions; it asks for pages back first; a shared prefix is held once and copied on write; a device’s f16 rows in and out.
    • crates/inga-model/tests/kv_paged.rs: the logits of Llama, Gemma 3, Qwen3.5 (its DeltaNet states per session), Gemma 4 (shared caches), DeepSeek2 (MLA) with paged caches, bit for bit, for every KV type (prompts by chunks, decode, a step taken back, the saved bytes equal); five sessions in one pool decoding together; a prefix shared by reference continuing as a fresh session; a full pool failing a step and leaving the cache as it was.
    • crates/inga-server/src/admission.rs: jobs wait for their pages, a large one is not passed forever. tests/server/openai.rs, paged_sessions_wait_for_pages_share_a_prompt_and_answer_as_alone: eight clients with one system prompt on --parallel 4 --kv-pages 12, each answer equal to the contiguous server’s alone, the metrics and the status.

    • CUDA and HIP (and Metal): the paged kernels (store, decode attention, the batched ones of batch.cu, the flash attention of prompts) and the pages taken before a recorded graph is replayed; the trait is there (GpuOps::kv_pool), their caches stay contiguous. Not attempted here: no GPU to run them.

H3 — batched prompts: a prompt’s chunks mixed with the decode steps (2026-10-22)

Section titled “H3 — batched prompts: a prompt’s chunks mixed with the decode steps (2026-10-22)”
  • The model’s mixed step. Model::forward_mixed(be, tokens, caches, s): sequence i has one or more tokens (tokens[i]) for caches[i], and row i of the result is the logits after its last token. Model::forward_batch is the case of one token each; both run forward_rows_of. Seqs::Batch gives each sequence its number of rows (batch_rows): the products, norms, feed-forward networks and routed experts run on every row at once, and each block’s per-sequence part runs a sequence’s rows as its own pass would (Seqs::One): RoPE from its position, the store into its cache (pages taken as written, H2), the attention (with Llama 4’s / Mistral 3’s temperature per position), DeltaNet and KDA (delta_seq), Mamba2 (mamba_seq), LFM2’s convolution (conv_seq on the sequence’s rows), Gemma 4’s attention and its shared caches. The arithmetic of every row is the one it has alone: the same bits on the reference backend. Model::mix_blocker names what does not mix (the batched step of these takes one row per sequence): multi-head latent attention and DeepSeek’s sparse attention, Gemma 3n’s streams, DeepSeek V4’s, Qwen3.8-Flash-Next’s, GLM-5.3-Flash’s and Kimi K3’s, diffusion models.
  • On a GPU. A GPU’s whole layers (RemoteLayers) run a mixed step’s decode rows as one batch (forward_batch, H1) and each prompt’s chunk as its session’s own pass (forward, the prompt kernels), the layers on the CPU between them on every row at once (stage_mixed); a GPU’s attention blocks likewise (attention_mixed: RemoteAttention::forward_batch and forward_span), the feed-forward blocks and experts after them on every row. No new kernel: a chunk among the decode rows of one device pass (the batched attention kernel with a row per position) is left for when it is measured on a GPU; the step’s composition and the CPU’s part are what H3 adds there.
  • Recurrent layers. Model::forward keeps a checkpoint before the last token of each call (what a new turn goes back to). A mixed step does not take it: the session runs each chunk’s last token in a step of its own after KvCache::checkpoint (prefill_mixed), the passes Model::forward makes inside one call, so the same arithmetic.
  • The batcher’s steps (crates/inga-engine/src/batch.rs). Batcher::prefill(tokens, cache, logits) queues a chunk as Batcher::step queues a decode step. A step runs once every session decoding has its step in (as before), and holds every decode step plus the oldest waiting chunk (one per step, so a chunk’s boundaries are its session’s own); a chunk alone runs as Model::forward (one pass for a recurrent cache, as in a mixed step). A mixed pass that fails runs again as the decode batch and the chunk apart, so an error stays its session’s. The spec’s “up to B tokens”: the decode rows (at most --parallel) plus one chunk of at most MIXED_CHUNK (512) tokens, --batch or --prefill-yield when smaller (--prefill-yield becomes the chunk size, as decided); PassGate’s turns stay for the prompts that do not mix.
  • The session (Session::generate): with a batcher that mixes (Batcher::mixes: Batcher::set_mixing, and the model’s mix_blocker), a prompt of tokens goes by prefill_mixed; a prompt holding an image or audio, a session weighing LoRA adapters (Phi-4-multimodal) or whose drafter reads hidden states keeps its own passes. The prefix cache (H2) is used before (use_prefix_cache) and published after (Session::publish) as before, and admission’s promises are unchanged: a chunk takes its pages as it writes them.
  • Settings and metrics. serve.mixed_prefill (on; --no-mixed-prefill) for inga serve and inga bench --users; the server’s log says when a model does not mix (Model::mix_blocker). Engine::batch_stats (BatchStats), summed over the loaded models: /metrics’ inga_batch_steps_total, inga_batch_decode_rows_total (the “batch size per step” of §1: divided by the steps), inga_batch_mixed_steps_total, inga_batch_prompt_chunks_total, inga_batch_prompt_tokens_total (the prompt tokens per step: divided by the steps).
  • inga bench --users N --long-prompt L: one of each count’s users reads a prompt of L tokens, arriving once the others have their first token; its time to the first token apart (long TTFT), the others’ ITL p95 the stall it causes. With --no-mixed-prefill, the same without mixing.
