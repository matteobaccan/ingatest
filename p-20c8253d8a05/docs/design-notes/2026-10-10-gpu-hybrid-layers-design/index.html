Hybrid models on the GPU: every layer kind, as much VRAM as there is

Status: proposed, 2026-10-10 (asked by the maintainer: “a version that uses as much of my P1000’s VRAM as it can”). First item of the 0.22.1 GPU work (TODO.md); built here, checked on lavapipe (Vulkan) and on the maintainer’s Quadro P1000 through the GPU runner. Shipped in 0.23.0: H1 (#287), the shared experts and dense networks beside their attention (#288) and H2 (#289), each checked on HostOps and lavapipe, their P1000 runs waiting for the GPU runner. Built in 0.24.0 (#301): H3’s Mamba2 / Nemotron-H part (§3.6) and step 3 of §3.1, the routed experts of the last layers on the GPU when they fit (§3.7), both checked on HostOps and lavapipe, the CUDA kernels and real cards waiting for the P1000 and the AMD card of #301. Open (TODO.md, the 0.22.1 GPU section): the chunked delta rule for prompts on the GPU and its speed on the P1000 (the rest of H2), and H3’s LFM2 short convolutions.

1. The problem

Section titled “1. The problem”

inga serve Qwen3.6-35B-A3B-Q4_K_M.gguf on the maintainer’s machine (Quadro P1000, 4 GB, about 2 GB free; 32 GB of RAM) prints:

A GPU stage today is the last contiguous layers of the model (RemoteLayers), and Model::plain_attention refuses the whole model as soon as it has DeltaNet layers (crates/inga-model/src/model.rs, “linear attention layers”). So a hybrid model (Qwen3.5, Qwen3.6, Qwen3-Next: three DeltaNet layers for each full-attention layer) runs entirely on the CPU, although everything but its experts fits in the free VRAM:

Part (Qwen3.6-35B-A3B Q4_K_M) Size Where today Where after this spec
attention: 10 full-attention + 30 DeltaNet layers, norms, routers, shared experts 748 MiB RAM GPU
KV cache (10 full-attention layers) + DeltaNet states 206 MiB RAM GPU
output layer 398 MiB RAM GPU
token embeddings (a lookup) 273 MiB RAM RAM
routed experts 18.3 GiB RAM / SSD RAM / SSD (2026-10-10-expert-strategies-design.md)

About 1.35 GiB on a card with 2 GB free: every product but the routed experts’ runs on the GPU, which is what llama.cpp does with --n-cpu-moe / -ot exps=CPU.

2. What already exists

Section titled “2. What already exists”
  • MoE split (CUDA P7, 0.21.0): attention on the GPU, experts on the CPU, the hidden state crossing PCIe twice per layer. The test mixtures_of_experts_split_attention_and_experts.
  • The output layer alone on the GPU (GpuStages::head_only, on the WIP branch wip/gpu-hybrid-layers, commit 36aea6c), with the placement written in words (“the output layer (layers 0-39 on the CPU: why)”).
  • CPU reference of every DeltaNet operation: Backend::gated_delta_net, the short causal convolution, the chunked prefill (reference::delta_head_chunked), the recurrent KvLayer with its checkpoint.

3. Design

Section titled “3. Design”

3.1 A plan per layer instead of a range

Section titled “3.1 A plan per layer instead of a range”

GpuPlan (src/commands/placement.rs) becomes a list, one entry per layer:

plus head: bool (the output layer). The planner fills the VRAM in this order, each step only while it fits (largest gain per byte first):

  1. the output layer (one product of n_vocab × n_embd per token, the largest of a decode step for small and medium models);
  2. the mixing blocks (attention or DeltaNet) of every layer, with their caches or states, and the dense feed-forward networks, routers and shared experts;
  3. the routed experts of the last layers, whole layers at a time (never on the P1000’s 2 GB for a 35B; useful on 8 GB+ cards with a 30B-A3B).

--gpu-layers N keeps its meaning (the last N layers), and --device / INGA_DEVICE too. The printout stays in words: “GPU: output layer, attention of layers 0-39 (10 full, 30 DeltaNet), KV cache; CPU: experts (18.3 GiB, 11.2 GiB in RAM, the rest streamed)”.

3.2 Stages that are not contiguous

Section titled “3.2 Stages that are not contiguous”

A layer whose mixing block runs on the GPU and whose feed-forward runs here is what the MoE split does already: one GpuLayers stage per layer, called by the CPU forward pass with the hidden state of that layer. Phase H1 generalises it from “MoE layer” to “any layer whose mixing block the GPU runs”, so the GPU and CPU layers can alternate in any order. Consecutive Gpu layers are merged into one stage, so a model fully on the GPU still pays one transfer per step.

3.3 Qwen3.5’s full-attention layers

Section titled “3.3 Qwen3.5’s full-attention layers”

They are not PlainAttention today, for three reasons, each a small kernel change:

  • gated output: q_proj gives queries and a gate; the attention output is multiplied by sigmoid(gate) before o_proj. One element-wise kernel.
  • partial RoPE: only the first rope_dim (64 of 256) dimensions are rotated; the RoPE kernel takes n_rot (it does already for Phi).
  • query/key norms before RoPE: present already (Qwen3).

PlainAttention gains gate: bool; plain_attention no longer refuses the model for sp.delta, only for the DeltaNet layers themselves (until H2).

3.4 DeltaNet on the GPU (H2)

Section titled “3.4 DeltaNet on the GPU (H2)”

Per DeltaNet layer and token: the input projections (in_proj_qkvz, in_proj_ba: ordinary matrix-vector products), a short causal convolution of width 4 over the last three inputs (a state of 3 × conv_dim), SiLU, L2 norms of q and k, the decay g = -exp(A) · softplus(a + dt_bias) and beta = sigmoid(b), then the delta rule on a d_k × d_v state per value head:

then the gated RMS norm with z and out_proj. New kernels (CUDA kernels/delta.cu, Vulkan delta.comp): conv1d_step, delta_step (one block per value head, the state in shared memory: 128 × 128 f32 = 64 KiB, so two halves on the P1000’s 48 KiB), and for prefill delta_chunked (chunks of 64 tokens, as reference::delta_head_chunked). The states live in VRAM; the checkpoint taken before a prompt’s last token (KvLayer::recurrent) is a device-to-device copy.

GpuOps gains conv_step, delta_step, delta_chunked, state_checkpoint, state_restore; HostOps implements them with the CPU reference, so CI tests the stage logic without a GPU.

3.5 Correctness

Section titled “3.5 Correctness”
  • Each new kernel against the CPU reference within the tolerances of the existing GPU tests (#[ignore], INGA_CUDA=1 / INGA_VULKAN=1).
  • A synthetic qwen35moe model (inga_engine::testing): greedy output on the GPU split equal to the CPU run for 64 tokens; a rollback through the checkpoint.
  • On lavapipe in CI for Vulkan; on the P1000 for both through gpu.yml.
  • The maintainer’s real Qwen3.6-35B-A3B: the same answer as on the CPU at temperature 0, and its speed against llama.cpp with -ot exps=CPU on the same machine (compare: llama.cpp).

3.6 Mamba2 / Nemotron-H on the GPU (H3, issue #301)

Section titled “3.6 Mamba2 / Nemotron-H on the GPU (H3, issue #301)”

Nemotron 3 Super (nemotron_h_moe, 88 layers: Mamba2, attention without RoPE and latent-expert layers, one block per layer) ran at 0.7 t/s on a Radeon RX 9060 XT with its VRAM unused, every layer refused by plain_attention. Each kind of layer now goes through the remote attention of §3.2:

  • Mamba2 (Model::plain_mamba, as the CPU’s mamba_forward): the input projection cut at upload into its rows for z, xBC and the time steps (three products), the causal convolution with its bias and SiLU (GpuOps::ssm_conv), the selective scan with the D skip (ssm_scan: one workgroup per channel, one invocation per value of the channel’s state kept in a register across the tokens, the sum over the state reduced in shared memory; d_state up to the workgroup size), silu(z) gating (swiglu), the grouped RMSNorm (group_norm), the output projection. The convolution’s last inputs and the scan’s states live in VRAM with a checkpoint each, as DeltaNet’s (§3.4).
  • Attention: PlainAttention::rope false, the model’s attention.scale.
  • Feed-forward layers (no mixing block): the squared-ReLU network with its biases (PlainFfn::relu2, GpuOps::relu2), or the shared expert of the latent experts; the router, the latent projections and the routed experts stay on the CPU, which adds the shared expert’s output (RemoteFfn::Shared). Such a layer goes to the GPU with its network or not at all.

The planner counts a Mamba2 block like an attention block (weights, state, checkpoint) and a feed-forward layer by its network (ffn_bytes); the placement says “attention of layers 7,16,…, Mamba-2 of layers 0,2,…”. CUDA’s kernels are kernels/ssm.cu (a module loaded apart), Vulkan’s ssm_conv.comp, ssm_scan.comp, group_norm.comp and relu2.comp. Left: the scan of a prompt chunked (prompts go token by token through the scan, which reads each token’s B, C and x once), consecutive GPU layers in one call.

3.7 Routed experts on the GPU (0.24.0, #301)

Section titled “3.7 Routed experts on the GPU (0.24.0, #301)”

A user’s 16 GB Radeon RX 9060 XT ran Ornith-1.5-35B-A3B (qwen35moe) at 10 tokens/s with 0.23.0, LM Studio at 20: every routed expert stayed on the CPU, the VRAM at 20 %. Step 3 of §3.1 is built:

  • Planner. Once every mixing block and the shared experts fit, what is left of the budget (inga_gpu::budget) less 128 MiB (EXPERTS_RESERVE: the products’ scratch, a prompt’s activations) goes to the routed experts, whole layers from the last one back, stopping at the first that does not fit (llama.cpp’s --n-cpu-moe N keeps the first N layers’ on the CPU). --experts-on auto (default), gpu (all, or an error saying how many fit), cpu (0.23.0’s placement); --cpu-moe N; --gpu-layers and --device as before (only the layers whose attention is on the GPU). GpuPlan::experts, experts_bytes, experts_why, experts_missing; the printout says routed experts of layers 20-39 on the GPU and the CPU with their bytes, and the tip how much more free memory would hold the others (or why they stay: a weight type, --cpu-moe…). The other experts stay in RAM or are read from the disk: the GPU, then the RAM, then the disk.
  • Forward pass. The router stays on the CPU (moe_forward). For a layer whose experts the device runs (RemoteAttention::runs_experts), each token’s k experts and weights go to RemoteAttention::experts with the feed-forward block’s normalized input; the shared expert is added after, as before. On the device (GpuLayers::run_experts): the routes sorted by expert, each expert’s rows in chunks of at most max_rows (8) through gate, up, the activation (SwiGLU, or the clamped SwiGLU of gpt-oss with its biases: GpuOps::swiglu_clamp) and down, then GpuOps::scatter_add adds each row times its weight to its token’s row of the sum. Only the sum comes back: one read per layer, as the attention’s. A decode step writes its token once for all its experts. This is ggml_mul_mat_id’s work done as one matrix-vector product per expert (llama.cpp’s MMVQ path for a few tokens).
  • Weights. Uploaded as stored, one weight per expert (views of the ffn_*_exps tensors), so every type the GPU’s products read works; a layer of another type, or an activation the device lacks (squared ReLU: Nemotron 3’s latent experts, beside its Mamba2 layers of §3.6; situ, Gemma 4’s scaled GeGLU), keeps its experts on the CPU and the placement says why. An upload that runs out of memory keeps the layers placed so far.
  • Left. Prompts through tiled products per expert (they go eight rows at a time through the decode products), the router on the GPU (one transfer less per layer), the memory planner counting the experts on the GPU before choosing RAM or disk for the rest, two GPUs.

3.8 Hybrid dense models by whole layers (issue #401)

Section titled “3.8 Hybrid dense models by whole layers (issue #401)”

A user’s 16 GB RX 9060 XT ran Qwen3.8-27B (qwen35, dense: 16 full-attention and 48 DeltaNet layers) under 3 tokens/s, the GPU 20 % busy, with “attention of layers 0-63, feed-forward networks of layers 36-63, KV cache and states of layers 0-63” there: step 2 of §3.1 put every mixing block and its cache for the whole context first, the networks in what was left. With a long context the caches took the room of the networks, and each of the first 36 layers went to the GPU for its attention alone and back to read its network from the RAM (one round trip per layer, §3.6).

For a hybrid model without experts the planner (hybrid_layers in src/commands/placement.rs) now tries every count of last layers whose mixing blocks go to the GPU, their networks then placed from the last layer back while they fit, and keeps the count that makes a decode step fastest: the CPU’s reads at RAM_BANDWIDTH, the GPU’s at GPU_BANDWIDTH, LAYER_SYNC_SECS for each layer the GPU runs, a cache counted at half its size (a session that fills its context reads half of it on average), the most layers on a tie. The first layers then run whole on the CPU (their caches in RAM), the last ones whole on the GPU, as a dense model’s; when everything fits nothing changes. On the simulated RX 9060 XT (hardware profiles, amd-rx9060xt-windows__qwen3.8-27b-q4_0-ctx65536.txt), --ctx 65536: layers 16-63 whole on the card instead of the attention of 0-63 and the networks of 31-63 (3.2 GiB of weights read from the RAM per token instead of 4.3, 48 round trips instead of 64); --ctx 4096: layers 0-1 whole on the CPU and layer 2’s network instead of the networks of layers 0-3. Mixtures of experts (the routed experts after every mixing block, §3.7) and Mamba2 / Nemotron-H keep their order, and so do --gpu-layers N (the attention of the last N layers) and --device NAME (every mixing block there, or an error saying how many fit).
