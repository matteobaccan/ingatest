Transcripts by speaker and time

Status: approved by the maintainer on 2026-11-05 (§15). For 0.45.0 (the item Transcripts by speaker and time of docs/todo/0.45.0.md, moved there from the backlog by the maintainer on 2026-11-04: “the file types to index by their structure, beside tree-sitter and the text chunker”; “with the minutes use case”). The audio reader of R11’s F3 (the RAG spec §5.3: “audio — symphonia decoding into Whisper / Parakeet”) is not built: this spec builds it, and gives its text the structure a recording has — who speaks, and when.

It follows the pattern of the structure of code (as built on exp/0.44-ts2) and of its sibling for mail, structured index: email and tickets: the structure kept in the store beside the chunks, the file’s indexing saying how it was read, tools for the model beside documents_search.

No code is written by this spec; numbers are estimates unless they say measured; claims about models, licences and sizes not read in this repository are unverified.

1. What the user gets

Section titled “1. What the user gets”
  • Audio (and the audio of video) in a collection is transcribed by Inga with the collection’s transcription model, its speakers found when a diarizer is set, and indexed by speaker turn, each chunk with its time.
  • Citations at the minute and the speaker: Meeting.m4a › 12:30 Marco; the web UI plays the recording from 12:30.
  • Speakers renamed once (S1 → Marco), everywhere in that file’s chunks.
  • Tools for “what did Marco say”, “what was said in the last ten minutes” (find_in_transcript), and the transcript read by time range (read_transcript).
  • Minutes from a recording: a use case and a prompt that writes attendees, decisions and action items, each with its minute.
  • The Documents page says “transcript · 2 speakers, 47 min” in the Indexing column.

Non-goals

Section titled “Non-goals”
  • Live meetings (a microphone stream indexed while it is recorded): the streamed transcription exists (--stream), but streamed diarization does not (C3 of the Canary / Sortformer spec). Later.
  • Recognising a person across recordings by their voice (a voiceprint): §4.4, opt-in, not in this spec’s first phases.
  • Overlapping speech transcribed per speaker (the multitalker Parakeet, C3): the turns here are one speaker at a time; overlaps go to the speaker who talks most.
  • Translation of the transcript; video frames (what is on the slides).
  • The browser page.

2. What Inga already has (read 2026-11-04)

Section titled “2. What Inga already has (read 2026-11-04)”
  • Engines (crates/inga-vision, behind inga_engine::SpeechModel): Whisper (whisper.rs, asr.rs), Parakeet (parakeet.rs, offline and streamed), Cohere Transcribe (cohere.rs); Silero VAD (vad.rs).
  • What a transcript is (asr::Transcript): the language, the duration, and segments with start / end in seconds and their text; with word timestamps (word_timestamps) each segment’s words with their times (Whisper by DTW over the alignment heads, Parakeet by its token times); Cohere Transcribe gives pieces of at most 35 s and no word times.
  • Speakers: NVIDIA’s Sortformer (sortformer.rs, built in 0.32.0, C2): per 80 ms frame the activity of up to 4 speakers, turns by NeMo’s post-processing, each word given to the speaker who overlaps it most, segments cut where the speaker changes (Segment::speaker, shown S1…S4, Transcript::speaker_text, [S1] in SRT and VTT). It runs offline on the whole audio, after the VAD’s time mapping; --stream --diarizer is refused. Files from tools/convert-sortformer.py (NVIDIA’s .nemo, diar_streaming_sortformer_4spk-v2 under the NVIDIA Open Model License; v1 is CC-BY-NC-4.0, as docs/use-cases/transcribe-audio.md says).
  • Whisper’s tinydiarize token (<|startoflm|>, asr::Specials::solm) is known to the vocabulary; speaker turns from tinydiarize models are not used today (to check).
  • Decoding (audio.rs, Audio::decode): WAV by hand, MP3 and FLAC through symphonia 0.6; channels averaged to mono; at most 30 minutes (MAX_SECONDS). M4A, AAC, Ogg and Opus wait for more audio formats (backlog); a video’s audio for that and video input, part 2.
  • The RAG: no audio reader; the Models page’s RAG section plans a transcription role (the RAG spec §9.5, “shared with Voice”, optional; part of R2, not built).
  • The web UI shows no file transcripts (the C2 spec’s “As built”).

So the item’s example, Meeting.m4a of 47 minutes, needs two things this spec cannot skip: M4A decoding and audio longer than 30 minutes (§3.1).

3. Reading a recording

Section titled “3. Reading a recording”

A new reader in the engine (crates/inga-engine/src/rag/audio.rs; the decoding and the models stay in inga-vision), called by the folder sync and by uploads:

  • Which files: .wav, .mp3, .flac first; .m4a, .aac, .ogg, .opus, .webm, .mp4, .mkv, .mov as the audio formats spec lands (the sync lists them as “not read: no decoder for M4A in this build” until then, in the Indexing column).
  • Opt-in per source: transcription is slow (below) and loads a speech model, so a folder source transcribes only with transcribe = true (the source dialog’s “Transcribe audio and video” box, inga docs add C DIR --transcribe). An uploaded audio file is transcribed (the user asked for it by uploading it).
  • Models: the collection’s transcription model and, if set, its diarizer and VAD, from the RAG’s transcription role (the Models page; shared with Voice); without one, the file is “not read: no transcription model” and the page offers to pick one.
  • Options: word timestamps on (needed to place speaker changes; Cohere Transcribe without them, its pieces given whole to a speaker), the VAD on when a Silero model is there (silence skipped: faster, fewer hallucinated segments), the language detected or the source’s setting.
  • Priority: the sync’s low OS priority (priority.rs); a transcription yields to chat requests between 30-second windows (the RAG spec §7.4); progress per file in the jobs (/api/inga/documents/jobs: “transcribing Meeting.m4a, 34 %”).
  • Speed, an estimate to measure: Whisper large-v3-turbo on a desktop CPU somewhere around real time to a few times faster than real time; Parakeet faster; on a GPU many times faster. A 47-minute meeting may take 10–60 minutes on a CPU (unmeasured). The job says its estimate from the first window’s speed.
  • Kept: the transcript as Inga’s JSON (segments, words, speakers, the model and its settings) in the store, so a change of chunker, a rename of speakers or a re-read never transcribes again. A new transcription happens only when the file’s hash changes, the user asks (Re-transcribe), or the transcription model changes and the user accepts (a model change does not re-transcribe a whole folder silently).

3.1 Long audio and M4A

Section titled “3.1 Long audio and M4A”
  • M4A / AAC: the audio formats spec’s isomp4 + aac features of symphonia (AAC-LC only), already in its plan; this spec depends on it for .m4a and video. It is not a new crate (features of symphonia, already in the executable), but it grows the executable (not measured).
  • Over 30 minutes: MAX_SECONDS protects the HTTP routes from a huge upload held in memory (16 kHz f32 mono is 3.84 MB a minute: 47 minutes are 180 MB; 3 hours 690 MB). For the index the audio is decoded in windows (symphonia’s packets, 30-second windows with Whisper’s overlap; Parakeet and Cohere on their own pieces) and the samples dropped as they are transcribed; a limit for the index of 4 hours (a setting), the routes keeping 30 minutes.
  • Sortformer on long audio: it runs on the whole audio today, with attention over every 80 ms frame (47 minutes are about 35,000 frames). Its time and memory there are not measured; the attention is blocked (QUERY_BLOCK) so memory may hold, but time grows with the square of the length. Two ways if it does not hold:
    1. windows of 10 minutes overlapping by 1 minute, each window’s speakers matched to the previous window’s by their activity in the overlap (who talks in the overlap is the same person in both): no new model, fails when a speaker is silent in an overlap;
    2. the streaming Sortformer’s speaker cache (C3), which carries speakers across chunks by design. The first is proposed for T1, with the second when C3 is built. The matching’s failures are said (“speakers of 20:00–30:00 matched by overlap with low confidence”).

4. Who speaks: the options

Section titled “4. Who speaks: the options”
A. Sortformer (built) B. Speaker embeddings + clustering C. Channels D. The user names them
What an end-to-end model: activity of 4 speakers per 80 ms a segmenter or the VAD’s segments, an embedding per segment (ECAPA-TDNN, WeSpeaker ResNet, CAM++), clustering a stereo or multi-track recording: each channel a speaker the speakers’ names, and splits or merges by hand
Speakers at most 4 any number one per channel any
Cross-file identity no possible (a voiceprint: §4.4) by channel’s meaning (agent / customer) by name
New model no yes: weights converted to GGUF, a new network in inga-vision none none
Licence v2: NVIDIA Open Model License (as docs/use-cases/transcribe-audio.md says) per model, below — —
Size a FastConformer encoder plus a Transformer (the 4spk models are about 120M parameters, unverified) 6–25M parameters a model (below, unverified) — —
Effort done; long audio §3.1 M–L S S

4.1 A. Sortformer: the default

Section titled “4.1 A. Sortformer: the default”

Built and tested on synthetic files (its DER on a real recording is an open check of docs/todo/real-world-checks.md). The proposal: use it as is whenever the collection has a diarizer, with --speakers from the source’s settings (default 4).

4.2 B. Embeddings and clustering: later, if measured worth it

Section titled “4.2 B. Embeddings and clustering: later, if measured worth it”

The classical pipeline (pyannote’s, NeMo’s clustering diarizer, sherpa-onnx’s): voice segments (Silero VAD, built; or a segmentation model), an embedding per 1.5–3 s window, agglomerative or spectral clustering with a threshold, the number of speakers found by the clustering. Candidates, all from memory and unverified (licence, size, the published files):

4.3 C. Channels: cheap and exact for calls

Section titled “4.3 C. Channels: cheap and exact for calls”

A call recorded in stereo (call-centre systems, many VoIP recorders, some phone apps) has each side on its own channel; multi-track recordings (a podcast, some meeting tools’ “separate audio per participant”) have one track per person.

  • Audio::decode averages the channels today; the reader asks for the channels apart (a new Audio::decode_channels, the same decoders).
  • Detection: two channels whose energy envelopes are mostly disjoint (the correlation of their 100 ms RMS below a threshold, and each channel silent while the other talks for most of the time) are two speakers; two channels that are the same sound (ordinary stereo, a room) are mixed as today. The source’s setting can force it (speakers = "channels").
  • Turns: per 100 ms the louder channel above the VAD’s threshold is the speaker; words given by time as Sortformer’s attach does (reused). The transcription runs on the mix (one pass), not on each channel.
  • Names: the channels’ default names Left / Right (L, R), renamed as any speaker.

4.4 Cross-recording identity (not proposed now)

Section titled “4.4 Cross-recording identity (not proposed now)”

A speaker’s embedding kept per collection would name “Marco” in the next meeting by itself. It is biometric data used to identify a person (GDPR art. 9: a special category), so: never on by default, only with option B’s model, per collection, with a clear consent text, the voiceprints listed and deletable in the local data view, never leaving the machine. Open question 4.

4.5 D. The user names the speakers

Section titled “4.5 D. The user names the speakers”

Always available, with any of A–C or none:

  • Rename: S1 → Marco per file (inga docs speakers C FILE S1=Marco, the web UI’s speaker chips, PUT /api/inga/documents/{id}/speakers). Stored in the file’s meta.transcript.names; the transcript JSON keeps S1.
  • Merge (S3 is S1 again after a pause) and split at a time (from 31:20 S2 is another person) as small edits of the turns, kept as edits beside the diarizer’s output so a re-transcription replays them when the times still match, and says so when they do not.
  • Suggested names (optional): the chat model reads the first minutes (“Marco, what do you think?”) and proposes names, shown as suggestions the user accepts; never applied by itself.
  • A rename re-chunks and re-embeds that file’s chunks (the names are in headings and in BM25, so “what did Marco say” finds them); the transcript is not redone. A 47-minute meeting is a few hundred chunks: seconds of embedding (estimate).

5. Chunks: one speaker turn at a time

Section titled “5. Chunks: one speaker turn at a time”

  • A turn is the consecutive segments of one speaker. Without speakers, a turn is a paragraph of segments cut at pauses over 2 s or every 60 s of speech.

  • Chunks: a turn is a chunk when it fits the encoder’s chunk size (500 tokens today); a longer turn is cut at segment boundaries into chunks of about a minute; short turns (a “yes”, “ok”) are merged with the next turn up to the size, the heading naming the first speaker and the chunk’s text keeping each turn’s label:

  • Heading: FILE › MM:SS SPEAKER (H:MM:SS past an hour), the speaker’s name if renamed, else S1; no speaker: FILE › 12:30.

  • Section (R2’s parent): ten-minute blocks (Meeting.m4a › 10:00–20:00), so two hits close in time bring their surroundings (the RAG spec §5.8).

  • Anchor: Meeting.m4a#t=750 (the Media Fragments syntax); the citation prints Meeting.m4a › 12:30 Marco. A chunk’s lines are the transcript text’s lines (so document_read and documents_grep work), its meta.t the start and end in seconds, meta.speakers those in it.

  • Filters (where, rag::filter): speaker (a name or S1), t (t>=10:00, t<20:00), duration, recorded (the recording’s date, below), language.

  • The recording’s date: from the file’s tags (ID3 TDRC, MP4 ©day, the WAV LIST chunk: symphonia’s metadata), else a date in the file name (2026-10-03 riunione.m4a, inga_docs::date_prefix), else the mtime; said in the Indexing tip.

6. What the store keeps

Section titled “6. What the store keeps”

As code’s code/NAME and mail’s mail/NAME:

  • The file’s entry in documents carries meta.transcript: model (name and variant), diarizer (sortformer-4spk-v2, channels, or null), vad, language, duration, speakers (count), names ({"S1": "Marco"}), edits (merges, splits), recorded, reader (TRANSCRIPT_READER, 1), words (whether word times exist), and the job’s measured speed (seconds of audio per second).
  • The transcript in transcript/NAME, one entry per file: the segments with their times, speakers and words (whether to keep the words is open question 6: about 30 bytes a word, so a 47-minute meeting of 7,000 words is about 200 KB, an estimate).
  • One entry per speaker per file: name, talk time, turns, first and last time — the attendees list of the minutes, and the Indexing tip.

7. Citations that play

Section titled “7. Citations that play”
  • The web UI’s citation viewer gets an audio player for a transcript’s citation: the recording from t (the chunk’s start minus 2 s), the transcript beside it with the turn highlighted and following the playback, the speakers coloured (the C2 spec’s §1 “coloured names”, never built).
  • Serving the audio: a new route GET /api/inga/documents/{id}/media, with Range requests (the browser’s player seeks with them), for a folder file read from its path under [docs] roots (never a path the caller names); MIME type by the decoder’s format. The same API keys and roles as the documents’ routes.
  • Uploads: today a collection keeps an upload’s text, not its bytes (to check in crates/inga-server/src/documents.rs). For audio, the upload’s bytes are kept in the store (store/media/HASH) so it can be played, deleted with the file; a setting turns that off (citations then show the time without playback).
  • A browser that cannot play the format (M4A plays in every major browser; Ogg Opus not in older Safari, unverified): the player says so and shows the time.
  • The chat and the CLI: the citation is text (Meeting.m4a › 12:30 Marco); in the web UI’s chat it is a link that opens the player at that time.

8. Tools for the model

Section titled “8. Tools for the model”

Offered with the document tools when a named collection holds transcripts, in the chat APIs, inga run and Inga’s MCP server (with collection required over MCP), results numbered as sources and bounded as the document tools’:

  • find_in_transcript — collection, query (optional, ranked by the hybrid search within the matches), file (optional), speaker, from, to (times MM:SS or seconds), recorded_after, recorded_before, limit: the turns that match, each with file, time, speaker and text, the anchor to cite. “What did Marco say about the budget?” is find_in_transcript(query: "budget", speaker: "Marco"); “the last ten minutes” is from: "37:00".
  • read_transcript — collection, file, from, to: the turns in order, bounded to READ_CHARS; the attendees (speakers, talk time) at the top. It is what the minutes read, window by window.
  • documents_search and document_read keep working (the chunks are text).
  • Each answer says the speaker labels’ source (Sortformer, channels, names by the user) and that speakers are a model’s guess unless renamed by the user.

9. The minutes use case

Section titled “9. The minutes use case”

“Minutes from an audio recording” (the item’s “with the minutes use case”): a recording in, minutes out.

  • A guide, docs/use-cases/meeting-minutes.md: record (formats, stereo for calls), add to a collection with transcription and a diarizer, rename the speakers, ask for the minutes; what to check by hand (decisions and owners are the model’s reading).
  • A prompt kept in Inga (a skill, skills/meeting-minutes/SKILL.md, as the skills spec describes them, or the web UI’s prompt presets): attendees (from the speaker entries), topics, decisions, action items with owner and due date, open questions, each line with its minute as a citation ([23:10]).
  • Long meetings: a 47-minute meeting is about 7,000 words, 9,000–10,000 tokens (estimate): within a 16k context with room, beyond a 4k–8k one. The skill reads it in windows (read_transcript by 10 minutes), writes notes per window, then the minutes from the notes (map, then reduce), and says it did.
  • The web UI: a “Minutes” button on a transcript’s row in the Documents page opens a chat with the file and the skill.
  • Output: Markdown (copy, download); later DOCX through the docs export.

11. Privacy

Section titled “11. Privacy”
  • Recordings of meetings hold voices (personal data), names, and what people said in a room they thought private. Everything stays on this machine: the audio, the transcript, the names, the index; deleted with the file and the collection; listed in the local data view (store/media/ for kept uploads).
  • No voiceprint is kept in the phases proposed: Sortformer’s speakers are numbered per file and forgotten; §4.4 is opt-in and later.
  • A transcript collection is personal as a mail collection is (the sibling spec, §8): not sent to remote models unless allowed per collection.
  • The media route serves only files of the collection, under [docs] roots, to callers the documents’ routes accept; the transcripts and names are not in logs or reports.
  • The use-case guide says what the law of many places asks (consent to record, telling the attendees), without legal advice.

13. Measurement: a new ingausecase case

Section titled “13. Measurement: a new ingausecase case”

A sketch for ingausecase, riunioni (written there when T1 is built; cases are on its cases branch, beside cases/email):

  • Data: recordings with a free licence and a speaker reference, downloaded at each run by dati.json (fixed revision, sha256), never in the repository, as rag-recupero does. Candidates, unverified (licence and whether the references are published): the AMI Meeting Corpus (CC-BY-4.0, English meetings of four people, word-level speaker references), VoxConverse (CC-BY-4.0, diarization references on YouTube audio), ICSI meetings; plus one Italian meeting of three people recorded for the case by the maintainer’s colleagues with their consent (about 15 minutes, its reference written by hand) — Italian is the case’s language and the public sets are English.
  • What is measured:
    1. Diarization: DER (missed, false alarm, confusion; collar 0.25 s) of Sortformer, and of channels on a stereo call, against the reference; the time and peak memory of the transcription and of the diarizer per minute of audio, by model and device.
    2. Questions (domande.json, 30 per recording set): by speaker (“cosa ha proposto Giulia sul budget?”), by time (“di cosa si è parlato dopo il minuto 30?”), decisions and action items, and two whose answer needs the speaker right (who promised what). Scores: the answers (the judge of inga eval), the cited time within 30 s of the reference time (recall@5), the speaker in the citation right.
    3. Minutes: the minutes of each recording against hand-written minutes: decisions and action items found (recall), wrong owners (count), lines whose minute is right.
  • Modes: (a) the plain transcript (inga transcribe --output txt) added as text; (b) the indexed transcript without speakers; (c) with Sortformer; (d) with speakers renamed by hand (the ceiling); models qwen3-4b and qwen3-8b.
  • The bar (estimates to confirm): (c) better than (a) by 10 points on the speaker questions; citations within 30 s for 80 % of the right answers; DER on the Italian recording within 5 points of NeMo’s on the same file.

14. Phases

Section titled “14. Phases”
  1. T1 (M): transcripts in the index. The reader (rag/audio.rs) for WAV, MP3 and FLAC, opt-in per folder, uploads; long audio in windows; the stored transcript; chunks by turn with headings and #t= anchors; Sortformer when set (window matching past its length limit, if §3.1’s measure needs it); meta.transcript, the Indexing column and the jobs’ progress; the transcription role on the Models page if R2 has not built it.

  3. T3 (S): minutes. The skill, the use-case guide, the Minutes button; ingausecase’s riunioni case run.
  4. T4 (S): channels for stereo calls and multi-track recordings.
  5. Later, after measurement: M4A and video as the audio formats spec lands (a dependency, not this spec’s work); option B’s embeddings if Sortformer’s limits bite; §4.4’s voiceprints if wanted; live meetings after C3.

15. Decisions of the maintainer (2026-11-05)

Section titled “15. Decisions of the maintainer (2026-11-05)”

The maintainer accepted every proposal (“ok, vanno bene tutte”):

  1. M4A: the audio formats spec’s AAC part (symphonia’s isomp4 + aac) moves into 0.45.0 with this item; T1 ships with M4A / AAC beside WAV, MP3 and FLAC.
  2. Transcription in folder sources opt-in per source.
  3. Long audio: a 4-hour limit for the index (the routes keeping 30 minutes); Sortformer past its comfortable length by window matching on the overlap, now.
  4. Voiceprints across recordings: never for now; only later, if at all, behind an explicit consent per collection.
  5. Option B (ECAPA / WeSpeaker / CAM++ / TitaNet): only if ingausecase’s DER and the five-speaker recording show Sortformer’s four-speaker limit matters.
  6. Word times kept in the store.
  7. An uploaded recording’s bytes kept so it can be played, a setting turning it off.
  8. The minutes: a skill shipped with Inga.
  9. The case uses public sets only (“usiamo dataset pubblici”): no recording of the maintainer’s; an Italian public set if one with speaker labels and a licence that allows it is found, else English only.
